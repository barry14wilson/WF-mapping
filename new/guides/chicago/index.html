Chapter One · The City

Chicago is a big, flat grid on the shore of Lake Michigan. Visitors spend most of their time in a compact area: the Loop (the business district inside the elevated L tracks, with Millennium Park and the Art Institute), River North and the Magnificent Mile just across the river, Streeterville and Navy Pier by the lake, and the Museum Campus to the south. The L trains, buses and the lakefront make it easy to reach neighbourhoods like Lincoln Park, Wicker Park, Chinatown and Pilsen. Creators suggest three or four days.

At Christmas the city puts on a big show: the German-style Christkindlmarket on Daley Plaza (in its 30th year in 2026), a 60-foot tree and free skating in Millennium Park, a skating ribbon in Maggie Daley Park, the Magnificent Mile Lights Festival parade, ZooLights at the free Lincoln Park Zoo, Macy's Walnut Room and Christmas Around the World at the Museum of Science and Industry. Families get huge, hands-on museums for cold days.

For solo travellers downtown is easy, friendly and walkable, and creators - women included - mostly felt safe. Plan for the cold (wind off the lake makes freezing days feel far colder), early darkness (sunset about 4:20pm), a Loop that empties in the evening, late-night trains, and a sales tax that is never on the price tag.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Chicago

Chicago does Christmas on a big scale. The Christkindlmarket on Daley Plaza, modelled on Nuremberg's and in its 30th year in 2026, opens on 20 November - the same evening the city lights its Christmas tree in Millennium Park - and the Magnificent Mile Lights Festival parade follows the next day. Add free skating, ZooLights, the Walnut Room and Christmas Around the World, and there is plenty for families. The flip side is crowds: in 2025 the city capped numbers at the market and queues formed outside.

Christkindlmarket Chicago (Daley Plaza)

The Loop · 20 Nov - 24 Dec 2026 · Sun-Thu 11am-8pm, Fri-Sat 11am-9pm · Thanksgiving (26 Nov) and 24 Dec 11am-4pm · Great for kids

A German-style market of wooden huts under the skyscrapers since 1996, with 50+ vendors (many from Germany): glass ornaments (including Chicago Bean ones), nutcrackers, gingerbread, bratwurst, pretzels, the famous raclette sandwich (about $17-18 in 2023-24) and Glühwein or hot chocolate in the collectable annual mug. Children get a free Kinder Club riddle passport, and there is a heated hall to warm up. A Krampus tent may frighten little ones.

Crowds: Very busy - queues of 20-40 people at food stalls and up to two hours for raclette at lunch. In 2025 the city capped the plaza at about 1,500 then 2,500 people, with queues outside. Weekday mornings are calmest. Creators found most food stalls cash only; ATMs are on site.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
The Christkindlmarket is packed on Friday and Saturday evenings and calm on weekday mornings. The tree lighting (Fri 20 Nov) and the Lights Festival parade (Sat 21 Nov) fill whole blocks - arrive early, agree a meeting point and leave before the very end.
Via A World Jo Traveled, Eat Local, Annika, Backttalk
Teen gatherings after big events
After the 2025 tree lighting, a large unsupervised teen gathering near State Street ended in shootings. Millennium Park now requires under-18s to be with an adult aged 21+ after 6pm Friday to Sunday, with bag checks at the Michigan Avenue gates. With children, enjoy the early evening and head back to your hotel as crowds of teenagers build. 2026 security plans had not been published by early October.
Via TheDC Shorts, LukeGee Travels
Pickpockets and card skimming
The Better Business Bureau warns of 'bump and skim' card thieves in holiday crowds. Keep wallets in front pockets, bags zipped and phones in hand, and never let a stranger tap your phone or card for a 'donation'.
Via CBS Chicago, Wolters World
Cash, mugs and prices
Creators found most market food stalls cash only, with ATM queues. Expect $10-12 for food and drinks and $10 for the annual mug (2023-25); $20 bought just a coffee, a bratwurst and a pretzel in 2025.
Via LeggLife, LukeGee Travels, A World Jo Traveled, Creatives En Route | Dev + Phil
Cold and dark
Sunset is about 4:20pm in December, and creators saw anything from 50°F with a -10°F wind chill to -13°C and snow. Most of the market is outdoors and standing - layers, hat, gloves, warm boots and hand warmers.
Via LukeGee Travels, Annika, Arbaaz Vlogs, Jiya Bhatia

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Chicago's reputation comes from gun violence, and that violence is real - 416 homicides in 2025 - but it is heavily concentrated in residential neighbourhoods on the South and West Sides, miles from where visitors stay. It is also falling fast: homicides fell 29% in 2025 to the lowest level since 1965, shooting victims fell 34% and robbery 36% (Chicago Police). In 2026 so far, recorded crime is down a further 9% in the downtown Central district and 7% in the Near North district that covers the Magnificent Mile. Downtown, the main risks for visitors are theft, pickpocketing and street scams, which cluster where the crowds are. Two things to plan for at Christmas: the Loop empties after office hours, and large unsupervised teen gatherings after big evening events have turned violent - on the night of the 2025 tree lighting, seven teenagers were shot near State Street and a 14-year-old was killed a few blocks away. This rating is for the city as a whole; check the area figures below for where you stay.

The creators

Most creators found downtown Chicago clean, walkable and safer than its reputation; several compared it favourably with New York, and solo women (LifeWithDayy, NK Medani, Izzy Bisges) felt comfortable by day and on the L. Locals keep repeating the same advice: the Loop is a ghost town after about 6-7pm, avoid empty train carriages late at night (the bus is safer because the driver can see you), and ignore anyone on the street asking for money for a ticket or a 'donation'. Pickpockets work Michigan Avenue, Navy Pier and L platforms. Creators who explored the South Side by day (CityNerd, Luis Gusto, Peter Santenello) found it calmer than the headlines, but advise staying alert and knowing where you are.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

The Loop after dark and after big events
Take most care: Evenings, especially after the tree lighting and other big events

The Loop is a business district that empties after 6-7pm, so stick to lit, busy streets. On 21 November 2025, after the Millennium Park tree lighting, a large 'teen takeover' gathering near State Street ended with seven teenagers shot near the Chicago Theatre and a 14-year-old killed on Dearborn Street. Millennium Park now requires under-18s to be with an adult aged 21+ after 6pm, Friday to Sunday, with bag checks at the Michigan Avenue gates. With children, enjoy the early evening and head back as crowds of teenagers build.

Magnificent Mile, Streeterville and Navy Pier
Take most care: Busy afternoons and evenings

Pickpockets work the crowds around Michigan Avenue, street performers, Navy Pier and L platforms. A shell game runs off the Mile in Streeterville, and the 'tap-to-pay donation' scam has been reported on East Ohio Street (June 2026). Keep bags zipped and wallets in front pockets, and never hand your phone or card to a stranger.

L trains late at night
Take most care: Late evening and night

The CTA says crime on trains fell sharply in 2026 after a security surge, but police data showed serious assaults on the system at a 24-year high in 2025, and creators still describe smoking, drug use and the odd attack on Red and Blue Line trains. At night, pick a busy carriage near the driver (the first car), avoid empty ones, or take the bus, where the driver can see you.

Via Tours by Foot - Chicago, Chicago Like A Local, Chism Prism, adulting with angela, ABC 7 Chicago
Union Station and transport hubs
Take most care: Any time

Wolters World says that on nearly every visit someone has approached with a story about needing money for a train ticket to Milwaukee or a bus. Say no and keep walking, or offer to go to the ticket office with them.

Bridges into the West Loop at night
Take most care: Late night

The West Loop is busy and well used, but the walk over the river bridges from the Loop or River North can be isolated after dark. Take a taxi or rideshare when alone. In 2025 the Fulton Market business association warned of fake parking attendants in hi-vis vests charging drivers $20.

Unfamiliar South and West Side neighbourhoods
Take most care: After dark

Most of Chicago's violence happens in residential neighbourhoods on the South and West Sides, well away from the visitor areas. Places visitors go - Hyde Park and the Museum of Science and Industry, Chinatown, Pilsen, Bridgeport - are fine by day, and creators who explored further found them calmer than the headlines. Don't wander blocks you don't know after dark, and keep nothing on show in a parked car.

Via Luis Gusto, CityNerd, Peter Santenello, VIP Trip Deals, Wolters World
Winter streets and the lakefront
Take most care: December to March

Snow and ice can slide off tall buildings downtown - don't stand underneath for photos. Wind off the lake makes it feel far colder than the thermometer says, and ice on the lake and harbours is never safe. Drivers are aggressive at crossings, and fast e-bikes use the lakefront path.

Via Wolters World, LukeGee Travels, Luis Gusto, CBS Chicago

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

The 'tap-to-pay donation'
Strangers ask for a small donation, often for a funeral, refuse cash and ask to tap your phone or card - then charge thousands. Reported in Streeterville, the West Loop, Lincoln Park, Wicker Park and Lakeview in 2025-26; banks can refuse refunds because you authorised the payment. The Better Business Bureau says refusing cash is a red flag.
Via CBS Chicago, NBC Chicago
Shell games and street pitches
Card and ball-and-cup games off the Magnificent Mile are rigged, and pickpockets work the crowd that gathers to watch. Downtown you may also be pushed to sign up for dubious subscriptions - keep walking.
Via Wolters World, CBS Chicago, Chism Prism
Money for a train ticket
Sob stories at Union Station and other hubs about needing money for a ticket home. Offer to buy the ticket at the counter with them, or say no.
Via Wolters World
Taxis
Some drivers claim the card machine is broken and ask for cash - check before you get in. After games at the United Center, unlicensed cabs and flat-rate fares ($40-45 against about $20 on the meter) were reported. Genuine cabs show a roof number that matches the plate; the Curb and Arro apps book licensed taxis.
Via Wolters World, ABC 7 Chicago
Fake parking attendants
People in hi-vis vests have waved drivers into private lots in Fulton Market and around Sox games, charging $20-40. Park only in official garages, or use the SpotHero app.
Via CBS Chicago
Bills, fees and upsells
The 10.25% sales tax is never on the price tag, restaurants add a 0.5% tax, and some add a service fee or about 3% surcharge - check before tipping. Deep-dish pizzerias push starters you won't need: even a small pizza is very filling.
Via Travel Tips Group, Brad Ball Travels, Rithmic ASMR, Wolters World

Chapter Seven · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around Chicago

From the airports
From O'Hare, the Blue Line runs 24 hours and takes 40-45 minutes to downtown; boarding at O'Hare costs $5 (free with a pass). From Midway, the Orange Line takes 20-25 minutes for $2.50. A taxi from O'Hare averages about $50, and the city's shared-ride flat fare is $30 a person (Chicago airports, Oct 2026).
Via Roaming Routes, Chicago Like A Local, Camden David, Everything Chicagoland
Tickets: just tap
A train ride is $2.50 and a bus $2.25, with two free transfers within two hours. Tap a contactless card or phone at the gate - no ticket needed. Passes are $5 for a day, $15 for three days and $20 for seven days in the Ventra app or at airport machines; the 7-day pass covers the O'Hare charge.
Via Chicago Like A Local, Camden David, CityNerd, Traveling Taya
Getting round the sights
Every L line except the Yellow runs through the Loop; Clark/Lake connects to all of them. For ZooLights take the Brown Line to Sedgwick or bus 151. The Red Line goes to Wrigley Field, and the Metra Electric from Millennium Station to Hyde Park for the Museum of Science and Industry. In cold weather, the Pedway tunnels link some downtown buildings and stations.
Via Luis Gusto, CityNerd, LukeGee Travels, Ashlynne Eaton, Everything Chicagoland , Tours by Foot - Chicago
Night travel
The Red and Blue Lines run 24 hours; other lines run roughly 4am to 1am. Locals prefer the bus late at night because the driver can see you, and say to pad journey times outside rush hour. Rideshares can be near impossible at rush hour - Caira Button gave up at ZooLights and took the bus.
Via Caira Button, Chicago Like A Local, Tours by Foot - Chicago, Chism Prism
Water taxi and bikes
The Chicago Water Taxi to Chinatown ($6-10, 2025-26) is a cheap way onto the river, but check winter service. Divvy bikes are $1 to unlock plus 20 cents a minute, or $19.90 for a day (Divvy, Oct 2026) - not much fun in a Chicago December.
Via Roaming Routes, Destinations2see, Camden David

Quick tips

Book the timed things
Skating slots, the Walnut Room, Skydeck and ZooLights all sell out at Christmas. One creator could only get an 8am skating slot over the 2025 holiday weekend.
Dress for the wind
Lake wind makes 25°F feel like 10°F. Layers, a hat, gloves, warm boots and hand warmers (from any Walgreens).
Carry some cash
Cards and phone pay work almost everywhere, but creators found most Christkindlmarket food stalls cash only - there are ATMs on site, with queues.
Tax and tip on top
Prices never include the 10.25% sales tax. Tip 18-20% in restaurants, but check for a service charge first.
Weekday mornings win
The Christkindlmarket, the Bean and the Walnut Room are calm on weekday mornings and packed on Saturday afternoons.
Christmas Day
The Christkindlmarket ends at 4pm on 24 December. On 25 December many museums, ZooLights and Navy Pier's WonderFest are closed - but the Skydeck is open every day and the Maggie Daley ribbon has morning sessions.

Paying for transport

How much is a CTA ride?

Can I just tap my bank card?
Yes. You can pay with a contactless bank card, a phone wallet, a Ventra card or a Ventra ticket. Tap-to-pay covers full and reduced fares only, not passes. The Ventra app shows balances, passes and train times. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Is a pass worth it?
CTA/Pace passes cost $5 for 1 day, $15 for 3 days and $20 for 7 days. They run from first use (1 day = 24 hours). A 3-day Ventra ticket can be bought at the Ventra machines at O'Hare and Midway. Checked 2026-10 ↗
