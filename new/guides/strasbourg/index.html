Chapter One · The City

Strasbourg sits on the French side of the Rhine, opposite Germany, and its historic centre - the Grande Île, a UNESCO site - is an island ringed by the River Ill. Everything visitors come for is on or beside it: the soaring Gothic cathedral, the half-timbered canals of Petite France, the Palais Rohan and Place Kléber. It is flat, compact and walkable, with trams for everything else; the station is 10-15 minutes' walk away. Creators suggest two or three days, plus a day trip to Colmar or a wine village.

At Christmas the city calls itself the Capital of Christmas. Its market, the Christkindelsmärik, dates back to 1570 and is one of Europe's oldest; today around 300 wooden chalets fill eight market areas across the island, with a 30-metre tree on Place Kléber that lights up to music every hour after dark. For families there is a free Advent Village with storytelling and workshops, carousels, a tourist train, bear-covered streets and a heated baby-changing area.

For solo travellers Strasbourg feels safe and friendly, and the trams are easy. Plan for heavy crowds at weekends, bag checks on the bridges, a market that closes at 9pm and restaurants that close earlier than you might expect, hotel prices that can triple in December, and cold, damp weather.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Strasbourg

Strasbourg's Christkindelsmärik dates back to 1570, which makes it one of the oldest Christmas markets in Europe, and the city now bills itself as the Capital of Christmas. Around 300 chalets fill eight market areas on the Grande Île, each with its own character, linked by streets decorated with lights, bears and stars. The 2026 market opens on Friday 27 November. It is very crowded - 3.4 million visitors in 2024 - and protected by a heavy security operation, with checkpoints on every bridge onto the island.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
The city says Mondays, Tuesdays and mornings are quietest and that Wednesday afternoons, Friday evenings and weekends are very crowded; creators found 10am-4pm hectic and after 8-9pm calmer. The first weekend and the last two weekends before Christmas are the busiest.
Via Sebby Fung, WonderJourneys, FlorianOnAir
Security on the bridges
In 2025 more than 1,000 people a day worked on market security - police, gendarmes, Sentinelle soldiers, private guards and drone monitoring - with checks at every bridge onto the island, bulky bags searched, and cars banned during market hours. Creators found the armed patrols intense-looking but reassuring. The 2026 plan is usually announced in mid-November.
Via Tourist Walk Tours, Sebby Fung, Grant & Teresa
Remembering 2018
On 11 December 2018 a gunman killed five people at the Christmas market. A memorial stele stands at the foot of the Palais du Rhin on Place de la République. The UK Foreign Office says terrorists are very likely to try to attack in France; stay alert and follow police instructions.
Source: SSMSI / Préfecture du Bas-Rhin
Pickpockets
A tour guide warned of pickpockets in the cathedral and Petite France. The Foreign Office says gangs work by distraction. Keep valuables in separate inner pockets and bags across your body.
Via Ivan de Guzman
Cups, cash and food

Carry small notes - some stalls are cash only or have a €10 card minimum. In 2022 the city asked stalls to sell regional food and set up standing areas behind the chalets for eating.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Strasbourg is a compact, well-policed city where creators - solo women included - felt safe day and night. The main risk for visitors is theft in crowds. French police recorded 25.8 thefts without violence (which includes pickpocketing) per 1,000 residents in 2025, up 16% on 2024: more than twice the Bas-Rhin and French averages, but well below Paris (45.1), Lyon (39.3) and Bordeaux (39.1). Rates are per resident, and the city draws more than three million market visitors. Violent theft without a weapon and assault are rarer but also above the regional average, and street robbery rose in 2025. At Christmas, security is heavy: more than 1,000 people a day work on market security, every bridge onto the Grande Île has a checkpoint, and cars are banned from the island during market hours. The UK Foreign Office says terrorists are very likely to try to attack in France; a gunman killed five people at the market in December 2018. This rating is for the city as a whole: France does not publish street-level crime data.

The creators

Creators repeatedly call Strasbourg very safe. Several solo women walked through Petite France and around the cathedral until 11pm without worry, and older solo travellers found it friendly and easy. The visible armed patrols and bag checks at the market struck some as intense but reassuring. The complaints are crowds - the first weekend and the last two weekends before Christmas are shoulder to shoulder - pickpocket warnings from a tour guide at the cathedral and in Petite France, fast cyclists in pedestrian streets, hotel prices of €400-600 a night, and restaurants that close early.

What the official figures say

Figures are from the SSMSI, the French Interior Ministry's statistics service, for the commune of Strasbourg (about 294,000 residents), published in July 2026. Rates are offences recorded by police per 1,000 residents (burglary per 1,000 homes) and do not count visitors. 2025 in Strasbourg: thefts without violence 25.8 (+16%), thefts from vehicles 6.9 (+32%), violent theft without a weapon 1.3 (+26%), assault outside the family 4.9 (-3%), home burglary 4.3 (-4%), sexual violence 2.2 (+20%). France publishes no street-level crime data, so this guide's map shows the markets, sights and the places named in official or press reports. On New Year's Eve 2025/26, fireworks and petards were banned across Bas-Rhin, under-16s on their own had a curfew, police cleared Place Kléber of fireworks just after midnight, and press counted 117-123 cars burnt across the wider area, mostly in outer districts.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Cathedral square and the Grand Sapin crowds
Take most care: Afternoons, evenings and weekends

The busiest spots in the city at Christmas. A tour guide warned of pickpockets inside the cathedral and in Petite France (2025), and the UK Foreign Office says pickpockets in France work in gangs - one distracts you while another takes from your bag. Keep bags zipped and across your body, phones in front pockets, and agree a meeting point with children.

Via Ivan de Guzman, Marcus and Chelsey, Ece Yılmaz
Bridge checkpoints onto the Grande Île
Take most care: Market hours (11:30-21:00)

Every bridge onto the island has a security check during the market, run by private guards with gendarmes. Large objects and very bulky bags are searched, so leave big bags at the hotel - creators had large luggage stopped on the walk from the station. Some bridges can be closed at short notice on the Prefect's orders, and in 2025 four tram stops on the island were not served during market hours.

Via Sebby Fung, Grant & Teresa
Petite France bridges in the evening
Take most care: Evenings and weekends

Some of the small bridges become almost impossible to cross at peak times. The riverside paths and wider streets give the same views with more space; go early in the morning or after 8-9pm.

Via Ece Yılmaz, Travel Time - Dj0rdje1, Alp Galip Travels
Cycle lanes and pedestrian streets
Take most care: Always

Strasbourg has France's biggest cycle network and bikes also use the pedestrian zones. Hold your line when you hear a bell and keep children close.

Via Wolters World
Place Kléber and outer districts on New Year's Eve
Take most care: 31 December night

On New Year's Eve 2025/26 police cleared Place Kléber of people firing fireworks just after midnight, and press counted more than 100 cars burnt across the wider area, mostly in outer districts. Fireworks were banned in Bas-Rhin. The 2026/27 rules are not out yet.

Source: SSMSI / Préfecture du Bas-Rhin

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Pickpockets in the market crowds
The main risk. Keep valuables in separate pockets, bags across your body and phones in front. Colmar's crowded lanes have the same problem.
Via Ivan de Guzman, Globetrotting Graeme
Cup collectors
Every drink comes in a cup with a €1 deposit. People may ask for your empty cups to cash in the deposits - you can give them away, return them, or drop them in a charity box.
Via Traveling Expats, Flora and Note
Tourist-trap stalls
Some chalets sell mass-produced goods, and identical wooden items sell at different prices on different stalls. Look for named Alsatian makers - the small producers' market in Petite France is the safest bet - and compare before you buy.
Via FlorianOnAir, Sophie Nadeau, Sebby Fung
Card minimums and big notes
Some stalls take cash only or set a €10 card minimum, and vendors refused a €100 note. Carry small notes and coins.
Via Seeking Paradise Bugs, Miss Mina, Traveling Expats
Chapter Four · Where to base yourself
Neighbourhoods

Where to stay

Pick an area first, then a hotel. The old town is compact, so most bases are within a short walk of the markets.

Grande Île (the old town)
First-timers · Christmas markets · Families

Inside the island, steps from the cathedral and the markets. Creators loved waking up in the middle of it, but it is the most expensive area - €400-600 a night at Christmas, booked months ahead - and the island is closed to cars during market hours, so expect to wheel your luggage from the tram or station.

Via Alp Galip Travels, Mandy Roams, alliepiki, Sebby Fung
Petite France
Couples · Families · Scenery

The canal quarter at the west end of the island, with half-timbered houses, the covered bridges and the quietest markets (the Advent Village and small producers). Mid-range hotels ran about €100-200 a night outside the busiest dates (2024). Very crowded in the evenings, peaceful late at night.

Via Tourist Walk Tours, TravelSpotter, Wiki Peaks, Noë Rose Travels
Quartier Gare (by the station)
Arriving by train · Budget · Day trips

Modern hotels opposite the station, a flat 10-15 minute walk along pedestrian streets to the markets, and trains to Colmar every half hour. Best Western Plus Monopol Métropole was about $200 a night for two with breakfast (2025). Large luggage is checked on the walk into the old town during the market.

Via WithLoah - World, Travel with NJ Steve & Sue!, Sebby Fung, where is kat
Krutenau
Solo travellers · Young · Food

The student quarter just east of the island, with cafés, bars and cheaper eats. A few minutes' walk to the cathedral.

Via Miss Mina, Le Routin, Sophie Nadeau
Neustadt & the European quarter
Quiet nights · Families · Parks

The grand German-era boulevards north-east of the island, near the Palais du Rhin, Parc de l'Orangerie with its storks and the European Parliament. Quieter and often cheaper, 10-20 minutes' walk or a short tram ride to the markets.

Via Alice & Lucas Travel, Nomad Flow, Alp Galip Travels
Further out on the tram
Budget · Driving

Alp Galip found a room for about €220 roughly ten minutes from the centre by tram when central hotels were €500-600 (2025). If you drive, the park-and-ride at Baggersee costs €4.20 a day including the tram for up to seven people.

Via Alp Galip Travels, Traveling Expats

Chapter Five · The Sights
What creators rate

Ten things worth your time

Ranked by how often creators recommend them. Almost everything is on the Grande Île and walkable in a day; in December do the cathedral and Petite France early, before the market opens at 11:30.

01

Strasbourg Cathedral

Grande Île · Great for kids

The rose-pink Gothic cathedral, 142 m tall and once the tallest building in the world. Entry is free (via the south door during the market, with a bag check). At Christmas it hangs 14 tapestries of the life of the Virgin and a large nativity. Note: the famous astronomical clock show is not held during the market. Closed to visitors on Christmas Day.

Where to eat

Alsatian food is hearty and half German: tarte flambée (flammekueche, a thin crust with cream, onion and bacon), choucroute with several meats, spätzle, baeckeoffe, pretzels and kougelhopf. Eat in a winstub, and book: restaurants fill at Christmas and many kitchens close around 2pm and stop early in the evening. Prices carry the year.

Chapter Seven · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around Strasbourg

Getting there
From London, Eurostar to Paris then the TGV from Gare de l'Est: about 6 hours in all, and the TGV leg takes about 1h45-2h - book early, as December trains fill up. From Strasbourg-Entzheim airport a train runs to the station in 8 minutes (€3.20, 2026). From Basel-Mulhouse airport take the shuttle bus to Saint-Louis station, then the train.
Via Ninaderena, Mandy Roams, Our Bucketlist Journal, Les Frenchies
Trams and tickets
A single is €1.90 on the CTS app or a CTS contactless ticket card (€2.50 bought on the bus; tapping a bank card is not confirmed), and the 24h Trio pass covers 1-3 people for €10.20 (2026), including the tram to Kehl in Germany. Under-4s ride free. Validate before you ride. During the market some island stops are skipped - use Gallia, Gare Centrale or République.
Via Seeking Paradise Bugs, Pauli | De viajes y otros demonios, Tourist Walk Tours
Walking the markets
The station is a flat 10-15 minutes' walk from the first chalets, and the eight market areas on the island are all within 10 minutes of each other. Cobbles are uneven - creators walked 20,000 steps a day. A little tourist train runs for tired legs.
Via Sebby Fung, Grant & Teresa, Relax&Walk the World, Mandy Roams
Driving
Cars are banned from the Grande Île during market hours. Use a park-and-ride: €4.20 a day including the tram for up to seven people; Baggersee to Place Kléber is about 15 minutes.
Via Traveling Expats
Day trips by train
Colmar is about 30 minutes by TER, with up to three trains an hour; a return for two cost about €64 in 2025. Obernai and Sélestat are 20-30 minutes, and Riquewihr is reached by train and bus. Bus 21 or tram D crosses into Kehl, Germany.

Quick tips

Book early
Central hotels reach €400-600 a night at Christmas and sell out months ahead. Staying by the station or along the tram line is cheaper.
Go on weekdays, mornings or late
The city says Mondays, Tuesdays and mornings are quietest; Wednesday afternoons, Friday evenings and weekends are packed. Stalls open at 11:30 - see the sights first.
Small notes and the cup deposit
Some stalls are cash only or have a €10 card minimum. Every drink comes in a cup with a €1 deposit - keep it, return it to any stall, or donate it to charity.
Book dinner, eat earlier
Many kitchens stop at about 2pm and again early in the evening, and restaurants fill at Christmas. Reserve, or eat at the market.
Leave big bags at the hotel
Every bridge onto the island has a bag check during the market, and the cathedral platform has no left luggage.
Christmas Eve to 26 December
Stalls close at 18:00 on 24 December. City museums are closed on Christmas Day. The City lists the cathedral as closed on 25 December, but the cathedral's own feast-day hours are 14:00-17:15, so check before you go. 26 December is an extra public holiday in Alsace, so most shops are shut.

Local tips for Strasbourg

Paying for transport

What does a tram or bus ticket cost?
A single (1 hour) is EUR 1.90 on the CTS app, on a Badgeo card or when topping up a paper-style contactless card (BSC). Buying on board a bus costs EUR 2.50. Pairs: EUR 3.60. Ten trips: EUR 17.10 (EUR 15.20 for children 4 to 11). Checked 2026-10 ↗
Is a day ticket worth it for a family?
24h Solo is from EUR 4.60 for one person. 24h Trio is EUR 10.20 for 1 to 3 people, valid 24 hours from first use on buses and trams, including the line D tram to Kehl in Germany. First purchase on a contactless card adds EUR 0.20. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Do small children need a ticket?
Children under 4 travel free without a ticket. From age 4 a ticket is required. Validate it even if it is free. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Can I tap my bank card on the tram?
We could not confirm on CTS pages that a bank card can be tapped on the validator. Buy a ticket on the CTS app or a contactless ticket before boarding, and validate it. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Driving in: where do I park?
Park-and-ride (P+R) is EUR 4.20 and includes unlimited bus and tram for 1 to 7 people (parking valid 4:30 to 02:00 next day). The city centre inside the Grande Ile is closed to cars during market hours. In 2025 the P+R car parks ran 24 hours with an extra car park at the Zenith and a weekend shuttle. 2026 arrangements not yet confirmed. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Trams skip the market stops
In 2025 the tram stops Broglie, Alt Winmaerik and Langstross Grand'Rue were not served during market hours (11:30 to 21:00), and Homme de Fer could be shut when crowded. Use Gallia, Gare Centrale or Republique and walk, or the line A, C, D and F stops near the venues. 2026 changes not yet confirmed. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas How do I get from Strasbourg station to the market?
The first chalets are about a 10 minute walk from Strasbourg Central Station. There are official information booths at Place de la Gare and Place Kleber. Checked 2026-10 ↗
How do I get from Strasbourg-Entzheim airport?
Train shuttle to Strasbourg station takes 8 minutes, up to 5 trains an hour. A rail-only ticket is EUR 3.20 one way. With the CTS app a combined train plus tram plus bus ticket starts at EUR 1.90. An Alsa+ 24h EMS ticket (train, tram, bus, valid 24h in the metropolitan area) is EUR 4.60. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Flying into Basel-Mulhouse instead
A direct shuttle bus runs between the airport and Saint-Louis (France) station every 15 to 30 minutes from 05:00 to midnight, about 10 to 15 minutes. EuroAirport says direct trains to Strasbourg leave Saint-Louis every 30 minutes. Price and total time to Strasbourg are not yet confirmed on an official page (SNCF Connect lists Basel to Strasbourg from EUR 11, about 1h27, indicative only). Checked 2026-10 ↗
London to Strasbourg by train
Eurostar to Paris Gare du Nord, then a TGV from Gare de l'Est. Eurostar says the whole trip is about 6 hours on one booking. Gare du Nord to Gare de l'Est is a 10 minute walk or about 15 minutes by transport. The fastest TGV Paris to Strasbourg is 1h46 direct; fares start around EUR 25 on SNCF Connect (indicative). Checked 2026-10 ↗
Hiring a bike
Velhop rental without subscription is EUR 1.20 per hour, EUR 7 for up to 24 hours, EUR 14 for up to 48 hours. Late return costs EUR 14 for each extra 24 hours. The City says Strasbourg has more than 600 km of cycle routes. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Are the cycle lanes safe for kids?
The City says it has the first cycling network in France, with more than 600 km of routes and 6,000 shared Velhop bikes. Whether each lane is suitable for children is not yet confirmed; in December the pedestrian Grande Ile is crowded, so push the bike. Checked 2026-10 ↗

Water & snacks

Christmas What is the cup deposit on mulled wine?
Every drink comes in a returnable cup. The deposit is EUR 1. Hand the cup back at any drinks stall for your EUR 1, or drop it at a charity collection point and the EUR 1 goes to a Strasbourg charity. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas How much is a vin chaud?
A local guide blog (not official) found 3 to 7 EUR for a standard glass, most at EUR 5, plus the EUR 1 deposit, in 2025. A BFM report in 2023 put the 30 cl glass at EUR 5 including the deposit. 2026 prices not yet confirmed. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Can I drink the tap water?
Yes. The Metropole says its tap water meets bacterial and chemical limits and is naturally potable, with only a light preventive chlorination. Bring a refillable bottle. Checked 2026-10 ↗

There are many free public toilets on the Grande Ile, some accessible for disabled visitors. Use the free 'Ici Toilettes' app or the official event map to find permanent and temporary ones and partner businesses. The cathedral platform has none; use the public toilets on Place du Chateau. Square Louise Weiss (Petite France) hosts the Advent Village: daily storytelling, creative workshops, concerts and games, free. Money & tipping

Do I have to tip?
No. Menu prices must be the price you actually pay, service included ('service compris'). Tipping is optional. Leave coins for good service if you wish. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Is there a hotel tax on top?
Yes. A tourist tax is charged per person per night and shown separately on the invoice. For unclassified places it is 5% of the pre-tax nightly price plus 10% county add-on, capped at EUR 5.06 per person per night. Under 18s are exempt. Classified hotels have fixed rates (not confirmed on the city page). Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Do the market stalls take cards?
No official statement found on cash or card at the stalls. Carry some cash and a card. The EUR 1 cup deposit is refundable at any drinks stall. Checked 2026-10 ↗

Safety habits

Christmas What are the security checks at the market?
Visitors are filtered at the bridges onto the Grande Ile by private security with gendarmes in support. In 2025 large objects were searched automatically and random checks were possible. No ban on bags is stated, but leave big bags and luggage at the hotel. The 2026 plan is not published yet. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Can I drive into the centre?
No. Motor vehicles are prohibited on the Grande Ile during market hours, unless you hold a pass issued by the City. Walk, cycle, use public transport or the boat. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Pickpockets in the crowds
The UK Foreign Office warns pickpockets work in gangs, one distracts you while another takes from your bag. Keep passport, cards and valuables in separate bags or pockets, carry the bag across your body, keep items close in cafes, and do not get distracted around tourist sights and cash machines. SSMSI recorded 25.8 thefts without violence per 1,000 residents in Strasbourg in 2025, up 16% on 2024. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Terrorism risk at the markets
FCDO says terrorists are very likely to try to attack in France, including crowded places and cultural events. Stay aware, follow local authority advice and any police instruction. A gunman killed five people at the Strasbourg market on 11 December 2018; a memorial stele is at Place de la Republique. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Knives, pepper spray and self-defence items
Carrying a category D weapon, including a knife or spray, outside your home without a legitimate reason is banned. Self-defence is not accepted as a legitimate reason. Penalty up to EUR 15,000 and 1 year in prison (EUR 30,000 and 2 years if with another person). Leave them at home. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Is it safe to drive after a vin chaud?
France has strict drink-driving laws; the legal limit is a third lower than in England and Wales. Police run roadside checks for alcohol and drugs. Penalties include fines, loss of licence and prison. Drinks in French bars are often stronger than in the UK. Checked 2026-10 ↗
New Year's Eve in Strasbourg
On 31 Dec 2025 most petards and fireworks were banned in Bas-Rhin to 3 Jan 2026, under-16s not accompanied had a curfew in Strasbourg and six nearby towns, and 117 to 123 cars were burnt in the area depending on the count. Trouble was mostly between 23:00 and 02:00 in suburbs; Place Kleber was cleared at about 00:20. Avoid parking in suburbs, avoid fireworks crowds. 2026/27 rules NOT yet announced. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Must I carry ID?
Yes. You must be able to prove who you are when asked, or within 4 hours at a police station. A passport, photocard driving licence or other government document is accepted. Covering your face in public is illegal, with a fine up to EUR 150. Checked 2026-10 ↗

Christmas Are shops open on Sundays in December?
In Alsace-Moselle shops are normally closed on Sundays and public holidays, except types such as bakeries, florists, souvenir shops, takeaway food and shops in stations. In 2025 the Prefect allowed Strasbourg shops to open on the four Advent Sundays and on 28 December (11:00 to 19:00). The 2026 order is not yet confirmed. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Is 26 December a day off?
Yes in Alsace-Moselle. 26 December (St Stephen's Day) is an extra public holiday there. In 2026 it falls on a Saturday. Expect most shops shut that day. Market opening on 25 and 26 Dec not yet confirmed for Strasbourg. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas How do I avoid the crowds?
The City says Mondays, Tuesdays and mornings are quietest. Wednesday afternoons, Friday evenings and the whole weekend are very crowded. In 2024 the market had more than 3.4 million visitors. In 2025 some streets were one-way at weekends and the Place Kleber social zone was closed. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Do the opening hours change on Christmas Eve?
The City says stalls open 11:30 to 21:00 daily, but close at 18:00 on 24 December so vendors can celebrate. City museums close early (16:00) on 24 Dec and are shut on 25 and 31 Dec and 1 Jan. The City lists the cathedral as closed on 25 Dec 2026, but the cathedral's own feast-day hours are 14:00 to 17:15, so check before going. Opening on 25 Dec for stalls not yet confirmed. Christmas Are dogs allowed at the market?
Yes, on a leash. The City suggests mornings or early in the week because of the crowds. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Cathedral clock show at Christmas
