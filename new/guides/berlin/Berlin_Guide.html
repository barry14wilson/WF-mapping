Chapter One · The City

Berlin is huge and has no single centre. Creators describe it as villages grown together: the historic east around Museum Island, Unter den Linden and Alexanderplatz, the old west around the Zoo, the Ku'damm and the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, and rebuilt Potsdamer Platz in between. Distances are bigger than they look on a map, so plan by area and use the U-Bahn and S-Bahn. Creators suggest three or four days at least.

At Christmas there are more than 60 markets. The big central ones - Gendarmenmarkt, the Rotes Rathaus with its ice rink and Ferris wheel, Alexanderplatz and Breitscheidplatz - open on 23 November 2026, with calmer options at Charlottenburg Palace and the Humboldt Forum, a Scandinavian market in Prenzlauer Berg and a winter slide at Potsdamer Platz. For families there are skating, carousels, a flying Santa, the zoo, and good wet-day museums.

For solo travellers Berlin is open, relaxed and easy to get around in English, and creators - women included - mostly felt safe. Plan for the things that catch people out: pickpockets in crowds, plain-clothes ticket inspectors, cash-only stalls, shops shut on Sundays, early darkness (about 4pm in December) and a handful of gritty hotspots where drug dealing is open.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Berlin

Berlin has more Christmas markets than any other German city - creators count 60 to 90 - from the elegant Gendarmenmarkt to fairground markets with skating and a flying Santa. Most of the big central ones open on 23 November 2026 and several run past Christmas into the New Year. Markets are packed on evenings and weekends. After the 2016 attack at Breitscheidplatz, remembered by a memorial on the square, the big markets are protected by bollards, police patrols and, at Breitscheidplatz, a knife ban.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
The big central markets are packed on evenings and weekends. Go in the early afternoon on a weekday - Breitscheidplatz is best at 2-3pm - and come back after dark for the lights. The Humboldt Forum and Charlottenburg Palace markets are the calmest.
Via Somewhere We Go, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), MY Travel BF, Adventurous Alex and Ukrainian Girl Olena
Pickpockets: fewer, but still the main risk
Pickpocketing fell 14.6% in 2025, but police still warn about crowds, stations and supermarkets, and ran a pickpocket prevention week in October 2026. At Breitscheidplatz they advised leaving large amounts of cash at home and carrying wallets under clothing. Keep phones in front pockets at markets and on the U-Bahn.
Via Americans in Germany Drinking Whiskey, Island Hopper TV
Security and knife bans
In 2025 Breitscheidplatz had bollards, a mobile police station, plain-clothes patrols, a knife ban and private security; police said they had no indications of a concrete threat. Gendarmenmarkt bans weapons and bags over 15 litres. Knives are also banned on all public transport. 2026 security plans had not been published by early October; FCDO advises being vigilant in crowded places.
Source: Polizei Berlin / Senatsverwaltung für Inneres
Cash, cards and deposits
Many stalls are cash only - Potsdamer Platz and the RAW medieval market were in earlier years - while Gendarmenmarkt takes cards in its food areas. Mug deposits were €5 at Alexanderplatz and the Rotes Rathaus in 2025; keep the token and return the mug, or keep it as a souvenir. A normal visit with two dishes and two drinks is about €30 a person.
Via Adventurous Alex and Ukrainian Girl Olena, Colin and Meg, İlayda Şen, Never Meet Your Idols – mit Mischa Lorenz, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo)
Cold and dark
It's dark by about 4pm in December. Creators saw freezing days and snow, but rain is more likely. Most markets are standing only, so wear layers, gloves and waterproof shoes, and look for markets with indoor bars - Alexanderplatz has good ones.
Via Easy German, MY Travel BF, Ann, Lou's Home
Have a lost-child plan
Before you go in, agree a meeting point everyone can find - at the Rotes Rathaus market the main door of the town hall works well. A Wiley Fox QR wristband on each child means anyone who finds them can reach you straight away.
Source: Polizei Berlin / Senatsverwaltung für Inneres
New Year's Eve
Berlin's New Year is loud. In 2025/26 fireworks were banned in four zones (Alexanderplatz, Steinmetzkiez, Sonnenallee and Admiralbrücke), about 3,200 extra police were on duty and 35 officers were hurt; about 20,000 people at the Brandenburg Gate party had no special incidents. The 2026/27 rules are not out yet. With children, watch from a distance and stay out of the side streets after midnight.
Source: Polizei Berlin / Senatsverwaltung für Inneres

In 2026 most big markets open on 23 November. Gendarmenmarkt runs to 31 December and the Rotes Rathaus and Breitscheidplatz to 3 January; Lucia and Spandau end on 22 December. On 24 December the Rotes Rathaus and Charlottenburg markets are closed, Breitscheidplatz closes early and Gendarmenmarkt is free; shops close at 14:00 and the Reichstag dome is closed. Shops open on Sundays 6 and 20 December. Book hotels and the Reichstag dome early.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Berlin has more recorded crime per resident than any other German city in our guides: police counted 13,642 offences per 100,000 residents in 2025, against 11,362 in the Cologne police area and 5,798 in Munich. But the trend is down. Total offences fell 6.7% to the lowest level in more than ten years (apart from 2021), theft fell 9%, pickpocketing 15% and pickpocketing on trains and stations 25%. Street robbery fell 9%. On Eurostat's like-for-like 2024 table below, Berlin sits mid-table for theft (below Cologne, Düsseldorf and Hamburg) but near the top for robbery, assault and burglary. Most of the crime visitors are likely to meet is theft in crowds, and it clusters in seven places the city has named as high-crime areas, such as Alexanderplatz, Kottbusser Tor and Görlitzer Park, where police can check people without a specific reason. Some figures went up: recorded knife attacks rose 5.5% (police say partly because of a new recording rule), sexual offences 15.7% (mostly online abuse-image cases), and home burglary 24.5%. This rating is for the city as a whole, not street by street.

The creators

Most creators felt safe in Berlin, including women travelling alone: Travel For Phoebe felt more relaxed than in Paris, Amsterdam or London, a DW Travel presenter who has lived there for ten years says she usually feels very safe, and Mountain Trekker walked to the U-Bahn in Kreuzberg at 3am without worry. What creators complain about is grit: litter, graffiti, broken glass, grubby U-Bahn stations and open drug dealing at Görlitzer Park, Kottbusser Tor and Leopoldplatz. Several found Alexanderplatz loud and uneasy at night, and two solo women felt nervous on poorly lit side streets. Pickpockets at the Brandenburg Gate, and touts and the shell game at Checkpoint Charlie, are the most common warnings.

What the official figures say

Figures are from the Berlin police's PKS 2025 short report (presented 11 March 2026) and cover the whole city (about 3.7 million residents). Rates are per resident, so places with many visitors and few residents look worse: the police's own area table puts the government quarter at 46,179 and Alexanderplatz at 28,817 offences per 100,000, against 12,882 for Berlin as a whole. Since 1 July 2026 seven places are 'crime-burdened places' (kbO): Alexanderplatz, Görlitzer Park/Wrangelkiez, Hermannplatz/Donaukiez, Hermannstraße/Bahnhof Neukölln, Kottbusser Tor, Rigaer Straße and Warschauer Brücke. Police say this lets them check ID and bags without suspicion; it does not mean the areas are closed. Knives are banned at Görlitzer Park, Kottbusser Tor and Leopoldplatz (since February 2025) and weapons and knives on all public transport (since July 2025), with fines up to €10,000. Recorded homicides rose to 165, but police attribute 79 to a single series of suspected killings of seriously ill patients by a doctor. In July 2026 a vehicle was driven into the crowd at the Berlin Pride (CSD) event near Potsdamer Platz, killing one person and injuring 29 (FCDO, 30 September 2026). The interactive crime atlas was switched off on 1 October 2026; only the spreadsheet remains.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Alexanderplatz
Take most care: Evenings and night

Berlin's busiest square and transport hub, with the TV Tower and a big Christmas market. It is on the police hotspot list because of group violence, and has a police station on the square; a research group reading police data says most recorded cases there are shoplifting. Creators found it fine by day, loud and uneasy late at night. Keep phones and bags close, and step away from groups that are arguing.

Via DW Travel, Dutch Travel Maniac, MashtagBrady, Travel Time - Dj0rdje1
Görlitzer Park & Wrangelkiez (Kreuzberg)
Take most care: After dark

Known for open drug dealing; creators were offered drugs at the entrances. The Senate's night closure was stopped by the courts (final appeal rejected on 1 October 2026), so the park is open all night for now. Knives are banned there. Families use the playgrounds by day - just don't use it as a night shortcut.

Via Destination Well Known, Dutch Travel Maniac
Kottbusser Tor (Kreuzberg)
Take most care: Evenings and night

A busy U-Bahn junction with drug dealing and violence linked to it, on the hotspot list and in a knife ban zone. Destination Well Known advises families not to stay right here. Fine to pass through by day.

Via Destination Well Known, Kai Lentit Adventures
Warschauer Brücke & RAW (Friedrichshain)
Take most care: Late night at weekends

The bridge between the S-Bahn and the clubs is on the hotspot list. It is packed with people going out at night; keep valuables zipped away and avoid arguments.

Source: Polizei Berlin / Senatsverwaltung für Inneres
Hermannplatz & Hermannstraße (Neukölln) and Leopoldplatz (Wedding)
Take most care: Evenings

Busy, scruffy shopping streets. Hermannplatz and Hermannstraße are on the hotspot list; Leopoldplatz is a knife ban zone, where Dutch Travel Maniac saw open drug use after dark (January 2026). Not places visitors usually need at night.

Via Dutch Travel Maniac, David G
Brandenburg Gate & Checkpoint Charlie
Take most care: Daytime crowds

The two places creators most often warn about: pickpockets in the crowd at the Gate, pushy street performers and sellers asking for tips, and at Checkpoint Charlie actors in uniform charging for photos, fake Wall pieces and a street shell game. Go early or after dark when the Gate is lit and quiet.

Via Rok Goes Around, Exotic Vacation, JOSH_vlog, AFRICAN TIGRESS, Destination Well Known
Bike lanes on the pavement
Take most care: Always

Berlin's cycle lanes often run along the pavement in a slightly different colour. Step into one and cyclists will shout - or hit you. Keep children on the footpath side and wait for the green man (crossing on red can be fined).

Via Carter Sullivan, DW Travel, WorldwideWu, Ultimate Bucket List

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Plain-clothes ticket inspectors - and fake ones
There are no barriers, and real BVG inspectors ride in normal clothes. A missing or unstamped paper ticket costs €60. Creators heard of fakes demanding cash on the spot: real inspectors carry official ID, so ask to see it.
Via Ultimate Bucket List, DW Travel, Nahana, Carter Sullivan
Used tickets sold at stations
Strangers at S-Bahn and U-Bahn stations sometimes sell old or already-used paper tickets. Buy only from machines or the BVG app.
Via Americans in Germany Drinking Whiskey
The shell game
A street guessing game with a crowd of 'winners' - all part of the team. Seen near Checkpoint Charlie and in tourist areas. Walk past, and keep a hand on your bag while you watch anything.
Via JOSH_vlog, AFRICAN TIGRESS
Checkpoint Charlie photos and fake Wall pieces
The 'soldiers' are actors who pose for photos and then demand money; Wall fragments sold nearby are almost certainly fake. The free Black Box exhibition across the road explains the real history.
Via Rok Goes Around, Exotic Vacation, Rob on Holiday
Airport taxis
A taxi from BER to Mitte is about €50-60. Creators who live in Germany say some drivers don't switch on the meter; insist on it and check they take cards before you get in. The train is €5 and often quicker.
Via Americans in Germany Drinking Whiskey, The Wondering Englishman
ATM fees and drinks
Use a bank's ATM rather than Euronet machines, which charge high fees. In bars, keep your drink with you; DW Travel notes drink spiking happens in Germany and drugstores sell test strips.
Via Destination Well Known, Cara Celeste West, DW Travel
Chapter Four · Where to base yourself
Neighbourhoods

Where to stay

Pick an area first, then a hotel. The old town is compact, so most bases are within a short walk of the markets.

Mitte
First-timers · Christmas markets · Families

The most recommended base: walkable to the Brandenburg Gate, Museum Island, Gendarmenmarkt and the Rotes Rathaus market, with transport everywhere. Travel Tips Group quotes three-star hotels from about €120 a night and four-star €200-250 (2025). Creators like the streets around Friedrichstraße station; the area right on Alexanderplatz is busier and scruffier at night.

Via Travel Tips Group, Destination Well Known, Easytravel4u, TRIP XTREME, Island Hopper TV, Exotic Vacation, MultiCityTrips
Prenzlauer Berg
Families · Quiet nights · Cafés

Leafy, cobbled and full of cafés, a few tram or U-Bahn stops north of Alexanderplatz. Creators pick it for families and a calmer stay; it has the Lucia Christmas market at the Kulturbrauerei and the Mauerpark Sunday flea market. Destination Well Known's favourite area.

Via Travel Tips Group, TRIP XTREME, Destination Well Known, Easytravel4u
Charlottenburg (Zoo & Ku'damm)
Families · Shopping · Calmer base

The elegant west, with the zoo and aquarium, Ku'damm shopping, the Breitscheidplatz market and Charlottenburg Palace. Ultimate Bucket List suggests families stay here near the zoo. Nahana paid about €60 a night for a Premier Inn here in winter 2026, 20 minutes from the centre by train.

Via Ultimate Bucket List, Travel Tips Group, Easytravel4u, TRIP XTREME, Nahana
Kreuzberg
Solo travellers · Food · Nightlife

Young, alternative and the best area for food, from Mustafa's and Curry 36 on Mehringdamm to Markthalle Neun. Hostels to smart hotels. It also contains Kottbusser Tor and Görlitzer Park, both police hotspots, so pick a street in western Kreuzberg (around Mehringdamm or Bergmannstraße) if you want quieter nights.

Via Travel Tips Group, Lois Park, Exotic Vacation, TRIP XTREME, MultiCityTrips, Easytravel4u, Destination Well Known
Friedrichshain
Solo travellers · Nightlife · Budget

Bars, clubs, the East Side Gallery and the RAW site. Destination Well Known says it is a bit rougher than Prenzlauer Berg and best for travellers in their twenties. Warschauer Brücke and Rigaer Straße are on the police's hotspot list - the bridge is busiest at night when the clubs empty.

Via Destination Well Known, Easytravel4u, TRIP XTREME, AFRICAN TIGRESS
Near the Hauptbahnhof (Moabit)
Budget · Arriving by train

Modern, affordable hotels on the doorstep of the main station and the airport train. The catch: there is little around them, so you need transport for food and cafés. Mashtag Brady found rough sleepers and a police operation outside the station on arrival.

Via Destination Well Known, Easytravel4u, MashtagBrady

Chapter Seven · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around Berlin

From the airport (BER)
Trains leave from the station under Terminal 1 (Terminal 2 is a 200 m walk). The FEX airport express takes about 23 minutes to the Hauptbahnhof, about three times an hour; RE trains are similar and the S9 takes about 50 minutes but runs every 20. All need a Berlin ABC single, €5.00 (2026). A taxi is about €50-60.
Via The Wondering Englishman, Rob on Holiday, Ultimate Bucket List
Tickets and fines
An AB single is €4.00 and lasts two hours with changes; a 24-hour AB ticket is €11.20, and a 24-hour small-group ticket for up to five is €35.30 (2026). Under 6s are free, and up to three children aged 6-14 travel free with an adult's 24-hour ticket. Stamp paper tickets before your first ride - there are no barriers and the fine is €60. The BVG app is easiest.
Via Rob on Holiday, Rok Goes Around, Experience Berlin Tours, DW Travel, Seth in Berlin
Getting round the sights
The U5 links the Brandenburg Gate, Museum Island and Alexanderplatz. Bus 100 is a cheap double-decker sightseeing ride from Alexanderplatz past Unter den Linden and the Tiergarten to the Zoo. The U8 goes to the Wall Memorial at Bernauer Straße and the S-Bahn to Warschauer Straße for the East Side Gallery. Allow extra time: platforms are deep and the city is spread out.
Via Rob on Holiday, Rok Goes Around, Radical Living, DW Travel
Night travel
U-Bahn and S-Bahn run roughly 4:30am to 1am on weekdays, with night buses and trams in between, and all night on Friday and Saturday nights. Trams and buses run less often on Sundays.
Via Experience Berlin Tours, MOUNTAIN TREKKER, David G
Day trips
Potsdam's palaces are about 40 minutes on the S7 and covered by an ABC ticket. Leipzig is about an hour and Dresden two hours by train. The Brandenburg-Berlin-Ticket (€36.50 for up to five people) covers regional trains for a day.
Via Rok Goes Around, Travel Navigator, David G

Quick tips

Plan by area
Berlin has no single centre and a walk that looks like five minutes can take twenty. Group your days by area and buy a 24-hour or small-group ticket.
Book the Reichstag and TV Tower
The Reichstag dome is free but books up weeks ahead and needs photo ID. TV Tower slots sell out in December - book online.
Carry cash
Many market stalls, döner shops, bakeries and toilets are cash only. Use a bank ATM, not Euronet. Mug deposits were €5 at the big markets in 2025.
Sundays and holidays
Shops close on Sundays (except 6 and 20 December 2026, 13:00-20:00) and on 25, 26 December and 1 January; on 24 December they close at 14:00. Most museums close on Mondays.
Stamp your ticket
No barriers, plain-clothes inspectors, €60 fine. Stamp paper tickets before your first ride, or use the BVG app.
Mind the bike lanes
Cycle lanes often run on the pavement. Keep children on the footpath side and wait for the green man.
