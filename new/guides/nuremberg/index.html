Chapter One · The City

Nuremberg is a compact, walkable city: the walled old town takes about 20 minutes to cross, the U-Bahn runs straight from the airport to the Hauptbahnhof, and almost every sight sits inside or just outside the medieval walls. The river Pegnitz splits the old town in two: the Lorenz side by the station, and the Sebald side with the Hauptmarkt and the hill up to the Imperial Castle. Creators disagree on how long you need, from 24 hours to three days; two is the common answer.

At Christmas the Hauptmarkt becomes the Christkindlesmarkt, one of the oldest and most famous Christmas markets in Germany, with a separate children's market next door and smaller markets around the old town. For families it is a gentle city: a children's Christmas market with carousels and biscuit baking, a toy museum and a railway museum, and a castle with a 50-metre well that children can pour water into. The weight of history is here too - the Nazi Party Rally Grounds and the courtroom of the Nuremberg Trials are here too, and creators urge a visit.

For solo travellers it is an easy, low-crime city by police figures, and creators found it safe to walk. The risks are ordinary ones - dense market crowds, cold dark evenings from about 4pm, and a busy, sometimes rough area around the main station. This guide shows you where, using the latest police and city reports.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Nuremberg

The Christkindlesmarkt is the reason most people come to Nuremberg: rows of red-and-white stalls on the Hauptmarkt below the Frauenkirche, opened each year by the Christkind and visited by around two million people. In 2026 it runs from 27 November to 24 December. It is genuinely crowded - creators agree there's no truly quiet time - but by police figures it is calm, with about a dozen pickpocket thefts in the whole 2024 season and no violent offences in 2024 or 2025.

Christmas markets

★

Christkindlesmarkt

Hauptmarkt · 27 Nov - 24 Dec 2026, daily 10:00-21:00 · Great for kids

One of Germany's oldest and most famous Christmas markets - counts range from 147 stalls (Prowalk Tours, 2025) to around 200. Look for Lebkuchen, Drei im Weggla, handmade wooden toys and Zwetschgenmännle, the prune figures that are for decoration, not eating. Choirs sing on Advent evenings, and a balcony Glühwein bar with a heater looks over the square (cash only). Creators split: most found it magical, but MY Travel BF found the layout repetitive and oddly quiet, and others found fewer places to sit than in Munich.

Crowds: Packed on Saturday evenings and weekend afternoons; Here's Good found both crowded and say there's no quiet time. Early mornings and weekday afternoons are calmer, and one creator found a Tuesday night in November comfortable.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
The Hauptmarkt is body-to-body on weekend afternoons, Saturday evenings and the opening night. Mornings and weekday afternoons are easier, and the streets just outside stay quiet. Nuremberg police advise extra care in any crush.
Via Here's Good, EmiLou Day by Day, Ty & Kena, Walking Alice, Jm Banquicio
Pickpockets: low numbers, same habits
Police counted only about a dozen pickpocket thefts at the whole 2024 market and barely double figures in 2025, and plain-clothes officers work the crowds. Their advice still stands: valuables split between closed inner pockets, bags zipped and in front or under your arm.
Via Jm Banquicio
Cash, deposits and coins
Carry euros - some stalls are cash only and cash-machine queues get long. Mugs carried a €5 deposit in 2025 and plates €2-2.50, so check the amount at the stall and keep your tokens. Market toilets cost €0.50-1, free for children in one creator's experience.
Via Here's Good, Adventures of Matt and Nat, Ty & Kena, Prime Travel Time, Jm Banquicio
Cold, dark and nowhere to sit
It's dark by about 4pm and creators saw -1°C to 4°C in early December. There are few places to sit and no heated huts, so you'll eat and drink standing: wear warm layers and gloves - some stalls sell wool insoles if you underestimate the cold.
Via Here's Good, EmiLou Day by Day, Kara and Nate, Adventures of Matt and Nat, Tu Guía de Viajes, MY Travel BF, Jm Banquicio
Have a lost-child plan
Before you go in, agree a meeting point everyone can find - the Schöner Brunnen fountain on the corner of the Hauptmarkt is the obvious one. The children's market can get so crowded you can hardly move, and police reunited many temporarily missing people at the 2025 market. A Wiley Fox QR wristband on each child means anyone who finds them can reach you straight away.
Via Chasing Footprints
Leave the car - and the penknife
Police ask visitors not to drive in on busy weekends; illegally parked cars are towed. Knives are banned at large events, with random bag checks, and drones are banned over the market.
Via Mita in Germany

The 2026 market runs 27 November to 24 December, daily 10:00-21:00, with the Christkind's opening at 17:30 on 27 November (christkindlesmarkt.de); Prowalk Tours' 2025 video says it closes at 14:00 on Christmas Eve, so check before planning that day. The Sister Cities market ends on 23 December. Book hotels early - a solo creator found single rooms very expensive at Christmas and chose a hostel - and remember the castle is closed on 24, 25 and 31 December and shops close on Sundays.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Nuremberg is a low-crime city for its size. Police recorded 7,028 offences per 100,000 residents in 2025, close to the five-year average, and still rate that as low among comparable big cities; in the 2024 national comparison it was the second-safest German city of over 200,000 people. Robberies fell by a fifth in 2025. The Christmas market is calm by police standards: about a dozen pickpocket thefts across the whole 2024 season and no violent offences in 2024 or 2025. The one area both police and the city single out is around the Hauptbahnhof, especially its south side. This rating is for the city as a whole, not street by street: German police do not publish street-level crime data.

The creators

Creators describe Nuremberg as clean, walkable and safe: Cleared 2 Travel calls it very safe, and Near From Home says the car-light old town makes everywhere feel safer. The complaint is crowds, not crime - the Christkindlesmarkt is packed at weekends and after dark. One solo creator (Bule Barbie) felt more nervous walking alone than at home, even on a busy Saturday evening, and another (Jm Banquicio) was told by other travellers to wear her bags at the front; she had no problems. One budget traveller found a red-light street beside the city wall near his hostel.

What the official figures say

Figures are for the whole city for calendar year 2025, from the Nuremberg police's Sicherheitsbericht 2025, plus police press releases on the Christkindlesmarkt in 2024 and 2025. No street or district figures are published, and there is no city figure for pickpocketing. Rates are per resident, so millions of visitors and commuters push them up. Some categories rose in 2025: sexual offences were up 14%, mainly harassment and mostly in public places. Around the Hauptbahnhof police recorded 1,526 offences in 2025 (1,611 in 2024), which they call low in the long term; almost half were drug offences found by police checks, and offences inside the station are counted separately by federal police. The City of Nuremberg's own 2025 report has no crime counts and is more negative in tone: it describes a clearly tense situation on the station's south side (Südstadtpark), with open drug dealing, and nuisance on the north side and along the Frauentorgraben. Knife and cannabis ban zones and an extended alcohol ban zone now apply around the station.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Hauptbahnhof - south side (Südstadtpark)
Take most care: Evenings and after dark

The City of Nuremberg's 2025 report describes a clearly tense situation on the south side of the main station and the Südstadtpark, with open drug dealing and disputes between groups; police have added video cameras and there is a strong police presence. Police figures for the whole station area are lower than you might expect - 1,526 offences in 2025, down on 2024 and mostly drug offences found by police checks. Knife, cannabis and alcohol ban zones apply around the station. Travellers rarely need the south side: use the old-town exits.

Source: Polizeipräsidium Mittelfranken / Stadt Nürnberg reports
Frauentorgraben, Frauentormauer & the station's north side
Take most care: Late evening and night

One creator staying at a hostel by the southern city wall found a red-light street on the other side of the wall, visible even in the morning, and advises avoiding it; the city's 2025 report names the Frauentormauer for prostitution but says it caused no public-space problems in 2025. The same report does flag the Frauentorgraben, the road outside the wall, for late-night disorder from partying crowds, drug dealing near the Jakobstor and poor lighting, plus problems around the Königstorpassage by the station, which it says have eased. Walk in through the Frauentor and keep to the lit old-town streets.

Via Cycle Baba
Plärrer
Take most care: Evenings

A major transport hub on the western edge of the old town. The city's report calls it a long-standing focus of police measures for nuisance and drug use, especially in the underground concourse, though the city's outreach team could not confirm most complaints. Fine to change trains here; no reason to linger.

Source: Polizeipräsidium Mittelfranken / Stadt Nürnberg reports
Hauptmarkt during the market
Take most care: Weekends, evenings and the opening night

The square is packed at weekends and after dark, and creators say there's no truly quiet time. Police recorded only about a dozen pickpocket thefts in the whole 2024 season, but they still send plain-clothes officers against pickpockets and advise keeping valuables in closed inner pockets and bags in front. Go in the morning or on a weekday.

Via Here's Good, EmiLou Day by Day, Tu Guía de Viajes, Walking Alice, Andrew & Kait
Castle hill & the cobbles
Take most care: Wet or icy days, and after dark

The walk up to the Kaiserburg is very steep, with a choice of steps or a long slope, and the old town is cobbled throughout. Creators found it slow going with a one-year-old walking and tiring for a solo walker. Wear good shoes, allow extra time with a pushchair, and take care on the way down in the dark.

Via Apure Guria!, Ageless Restyling, Chasing Footprints, iwannaTV, MY Travel BF
Nazi Party Rally Grounds
Take most care: Rain, match days and late afternoon

The site is huge, exposed and mostly empty, with long walks between the Documentation Centre, the Zeppelinfeld and the Great Road. Creators found it unpleasant in the rain, and on a football match day next door the area was crowded and car parks were full. Go in daylight, wear comfortable shoes, and check the tram times back.

Via The Iron Armenian aka G.I. Haigs, Munich Tour Tips TV, Cycle Baba, The History Underground

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Petitions and 'free' gifts
A Germany-wide warning: people with clipboards, 'free' flowers or bracelets in tourist areas and on public transport, who then ask for a donation. Smile, say 'Nein, danke' and keep walking.
Via ZeroZeroFourNine
Fake ticket inspectors
Real inspectors carry ID, and a creator who covers German scams says fines are never taken in cash on the spot. If someone demands cash, ask for ID or walk away.
Via ZeroZeroFourNine
Deposits on everything
Not a scam, but it catches people out: drinks come with a mug deposit (€5 in 2025), and plates, cups and forks carry €1-2.50 each. Keep the token and return things to get it back. One Feuerzangenbowle stand's mug had to go back to the same stand.
Via DW Euromaxx, Here's Good, Jm Banquicio, Andrew & Kait, Adventures of Matt and Nat
Cash-only stalls and broken cash machines
Many stalls take cards, but not all - including a balcony Glühwein bar and a hot chocolate stall. Creators queued at cash machines or walked to three banks before one worked. Bring euros with you.
Via Here's Good, Ty & Kena, Adventures of Matt and Nat, EmiLou Day by Day
Tourist-trap tables
Don't eat at the first restaurant you see on a main square - look around first. For sausages, a sausage house just off the market charged €3.80 for three in a roll against about €4.50 at stalls (2025), and one old bratwurst house offered its cheap roll only until 6pm.
Via Near From Home, Here's Good, Apure Guria!
Chapter Four · Where to base yourself
Neighbourhoods

Where to stay

Pick an area first, then a hotel. The old town is compact, so most bases are within a short walk of the markets.

Around the Hauptmarkt
Christmas market · First-timers

The heart of everything: step out of the door into the Christkindlesmarkt, with the castle a 10-minute walk uphill. Several creators had rooms looking straight onto the square - 'you can't beat the view', say Devin & Z. Hotel Sax is right on the square. Book very early for December.

Via Devin & Z, EmiLou Day by Day, A Festival Called Life, Cleared 2 Travel, TripHunter
Sebald side, below the castle
Sightseers · Couples

The north of the old town, close to the castle, Weißgerbergasse, the Dürer House and the sunset crowd on the walls at Albrecht-Dürer-Platz. The catch is hills: steep cobbled lanes are hard work with luggage or a pushchair, and one creator's hotel had no lift to the third floor.

Via Traveling Expats, Easy German, Ty & Kena, Ageless Restyling, Near From Home
Lorenz side & Königstraße
Families · Easy transport

Between the station and the market along Königstraße, the main shopping street, with St. Lorenz church and the Lorenzkirche U-Bahn stop one stop from the Hauptbahnhof. At Christmas Königstraße is lit up and lined with stalls. The city's 2025 report rates the inner city 'stable' but notes some begging and rough sleeping on Königstraße and Breite Gasse.

Via Jubaer Talukder, Jayesh & Shreya, Rocky Road 4K, Up for a Walk?
Near the Hauptbahnhof (old-town side)
Trains & airport · Short stays

The most convenient base if you arrive by train or on the U2 from the airport: the Handwerkerhof and the Frauentor gate into the old town are just across the road. Official reports flag the station surroundings, most strongly the south side (Südstadtpark), so pick a hotel facing the old town and walk in through the Frauentor rather than around the back of the station at night.

Via Cleared 2 Travel, Apure Guria!, Traveling Expats, Travels & Magic Destinations - Orion Ember
Budget: hostels by the city wall
Solo travellers · Budget

Hostels along the southern city wall are five to ten minutes' walk from the station, and one solo creator chose a hostel dorm because single hotel rooms were very expensive at Christmas. Check the exact street before you book: one creator found a red-light street across the wall from his hostel, and the city's report flags the Frauentorgraben, outside the southern wall, for late-night disorder.

Via Jm Banquicio, Cleared 2 Travel, Cycle Baba

Chapter Five · The Sights
What creators rate

Ten things worth your time

Ranked by how often creators recommend them. Almost everything is inside or just outside the old town walls; in December, see the sights by daylight and save the markets for the evening.

01

Kaiserburg (Imperial Castle)

Sebald side · Great for kids

The city's landmark, on the hill above the old town. The courtyards, gardens and views are free; a ticket adds the Sinwell Tower and the 50-metre Deep Well, where a guide pours water in and it takes about five seconds to land - children are invited to try. Exhibition boards are in English as well as German. Steep walk up; best at sunset.

Via Cleared 2 Travel, Traveling Expats, Mita in Germany, Easy German, Britt and Mitch Worldwide, Kara and Nate, Ageless Restyling, A Festival Called Life, Near From Home
Grounds free · full ticket about €9-10 (2025-26)

Weißgerbergasse

Sebald side · Great for kids

The tanners' lane - about 20 half-timbered houses that survived the war, now cafés and small shops. Often called the prettiest street in the city, so it gets crowded in the evenings, at weekends and at Christmas. Go early for photos.

Via Easy German, Traveling Expats, Rok Goes Around, Mita in Germany, Jazmin Angelique, Brian and Carrie, Jubaer Talukder, Así es el mundo

Hauptmarkt, Schöner Brunnen & Frauenkirche

Hauptmarkt · Great for kids

The main square and home of the Christmas market. Spin the brass ring in the railings of the Schöner Brunnen fountain for luck, and be under the Frauenkirche clock at noon, when its mechanical figures perform. The church is free to enter.

Documentation Centre & Rally Grounds

Zeppelinfeld (south-east)

The Nazi Party Rally Grounds: the unfinished Congress Hall, the Zeppelin grandstand you can still walk on, and the Great Road, with a circular trail of boards in German and English. Creators call it chilling and sobering, and say it needs longer than a quick visit. The museum was renovating in 2023, with an interim exhibition.

Chapter Seven · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around Nuremberg

From the airport
The U2 runs from the airport straight to the Hauptbahnhof in about 13 minutes - but only every second U2 goes to the airport, so check the display. Creators found a queue of taxis waiting, so no need to pre-book one.
Via Jazmin Angelique, EmiLou Day by Day
By train from Munich and beyond
The ICE from Munich is timetabled at about an hour, but one creator's train ran late and took about 1h40; regional trains take about an hour and a half. A Bavaria day ticket for two was €32 in 2025, and a seat reservation on the ICE about €4.90 each way. There are also trains to Stuttgart (a direct one was about €46 in 2024), Frankfurt and Prague, and Cologne is about four hours away. Delays happen, so leave slack for connections.
Via Jm Banquicio, Jubaer Talukder, Travels & Magic Destinations - Orion Ember, Travels of Sarah Fay, Ty & Kena, iwannaTV
On foot in the old town
The old town is walkable end to end in about 20 minutes, and maps are posted on street corners. It's cobbled and hilly: one creator's last stretch to her hostel was hard going with a wheeled suitcase, and another's walk from the station to the castle took 30 minutes uphill.
Via Cleared 2 Travel, Near From Home, Jm Banquicio, Ageless Restyling, Brian and Carrie
U-Bahn and trams
From the Hauptbahnhof it's one stop on the U-Bahn to Lorenzkirche, then about 10 minutes' walk to the castle. Trams run along the line of the old walls and through the medieval gates, and creators found everything punctual. Day tickets are the easy option for more than two trips - see Local tips for current prices.
Via Jubaer Talukder, Jayesh & Shreya, Sacred Places Journey, Cycle Baba
To the rally grounds
Tram 8 from the station area runs about every 10 minutes and takes around 15 minutes to the Documentation Centre (2023); DW Travel says just under 30 minutes from the city centre, and a local bus takes about 20. You can rent a bike at the Congress Hall to cover the huge site.
Via Munich Tour Tips TV, DW Travel, The Iron Armenian aka G.I. Haigs, Nicole & Ryan
Tourist trains
A tourist train with an audio guide loops past the old-town highlights in about 40 minutes - about €9 in 2022, fun for small children. A 40-minute trolley tour was €10 in 2023, which one couple found steep.
Via Mita in Germany, Nicole & Ryan, Brian and Carrie
Driving at Christmas
Police ask visitors not to drive to the market on busy weekends and to use public transport instead. No-parking zones keep escape routes clear and illegally parked cars are towed.
Source: Polizeipräsidium Mittelfranken / Stadt Nürnberg reports
Easy day trips
Bamberg, a UNESCO-listed medieval town, is a popular day trip. Closer in, the Faber-Castell pencil factory in Stein is about 30 minutes by U-Bahn (€3.70 single in 2024); its English tour ran about €9 in 2024 and needed booking. The Playmobil FunPark in Zirndorf needed online tickets in advance (2022) and has an indoor area open in winter.
Via Bucket List Urban Adventures, Apure Guria!, Trailblazin Peppers

Quick tips

Sights by day, markets by night
In December it's dark by about 3:45-4pm. Do the castle and outdoor sights first and save the markets for when the lights come on.
Wear grippy shoes
Cobbles everywhere, a steep castle hill and wet or icy December pavements. Comfortable, grippy shoes matter more than smart ones.
Look before you eat
Don't settle for the first restaurant on a main square. One creator's traditional schnitzel restaurant opened only at about 4:30pm and booked up quickly.
Two days is about right
Creators range from 24 hours to three days. Two days covers the old town, the castle and the rally grounds without rushing, with evenings at the markets.
Vegetarians: plan ahead
Franconian menus are meat-heavy - Obatzda and Käsespätzle are the usual fall-backs - but the market has a vegetarian and vegan stall, one stall did a vegan Drei im Weggla, and creators found vegan sausages at the Handwerkerhof.
Leave the drone at home
The old town and the market sit inside a no-fly zone for drones, and police enforce a flight ban over the Christkindlesmarkt.

Chapter Eight · Local tips
The small things visitors worry about

Local tips for Nuremberg

Every tip here was checked against an official or trusted source (VAG, Deutsche Bahn, christkindlesmarkt.de, nuernberg.de, gov.uk and others) on the date shown. Rules and prices change, so tap the link to double-check before you travel.

Paying for transport

What is the cheapest ticket for one person for a day?
The TagesTicket Solo was 10.60 euros when we checked. It covers unlimited trips on one weekday or weekend day until 3 am the next day, and you can buy it in the NürnbergMOBIL app or at machines. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What's best for a family of four?
The TagesTicket Plus was 15.50 euros when we checked, and covers 1 to 6 people (max. 2 adults aged 18+) in the Nuremberg-Fürth zone. Great value for families. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Do kids need a ticket on the U-Bahn?
Children aged 6 to 14 pay a kids fare, 1.90 euros for a single (90 minutes, one direction) at time of checking. Buy it in the app or at a machine. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is the Nürnberg Card worth it?
It costs 38 euros for adults and 12 euros for children aged 6 to 11 (under 6 free) for 48 hours. It includes public transport in the region and 30+ museums, including the Imperial Castle, so it pays off if you do several sights. Prices were current when we checked. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I get from the airport to the centre?
Take the U2 straight to the Hauptbahnhof, about 13 minutes. Note that only every second U2 goes to the airport, so check the display before boarding. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Should I buy a Deutschlandticket for a short trip?
Probably not. It is 63 euros a month and sold as a monthly subscription, cancellable by the 10th, so day tickets are simpler for a short break. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is the U-Bahn at Lorenzkirche step-free?
Not fully. The lift there reaches only the underground passage, not street level. Hauptbahnhof and Weisser Turm are better if you have a pushchair or wheelchair. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Water & snacks

Is the tap water safe to drink?
Yes. N-ERGIE says Nuremberg's tap water can be drunk without hesitation, even for baby formula, so fill your bottle at the hotel tap and save a few euros. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What is the bottle deposit thing on drinks?
Most plastic bottles and cans carry a 25 cent Pfand. Take them back to any shop that sells drinks, usually to the machine near the entrance, and keep the receipt to use at the till. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Are glass bottles charged a deposit too?
Yes. Reusable glass bottles usually carry 8 to 15 cents, and shops may only take back their own brands. Easiest is to return them where you bought them. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Do I pay extra for my Glühwein mug?
Yes, there is a mug deposit (Pfand). It was 5 euros at the 2025 market, so expect something similar and check the amount at the stall. Hand the mug back to any stall to get your deposit back. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Toilets

Christmas Where are the toilets at the Christmas market?
Public toilets are in the Rathaus (about 50 cents) and a paid toilet wagon stands on Obstmarkt near the Frauenkirche. The market FAQ also points to toilets behind the Frauenkirche, so have coins ready. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is there an accessible toilet near the market?
Yes, there is an accessible toilet on the west side of the Rathaus, just north of the Christkindlesmarkt. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Will I need coins for public toilets?
Often, yes. Public toilets at the Christmas market are pay-to-use, so keep a few small coins in your pocket, especially with children. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Parks & play

Where can the kids let off steam in town?
VAG lists central playgrounds such as Veit-Stoss-Platz (U1 Gostenhof), the Annapark with a water play area (U1 Maffeiplatz) and Luitpoldhain with a zip line (bus 45/65). Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is there something for kids at the Christmas market?
The Kinderweihnacht on Hans-Sachs-Platz has a carousel, steam train and mini Ferris wheel. The Christkind visits Tuesday to Friday at 14:30. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What can we do with kids on a wet day?
The tourist board suggests the Toy Museum, Planetarium, Playmobil FunPark and the Zoo. A free children's city map is available at tourist information centres and hotels. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Money & tipping

How much should I tip in restaurants?
Around 5 to 10 percent, or just round up. Tell the server the total you want to pay when settling up rather than leaving coins on the table. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Can I pay by card at the Christmas market?
Stalls generally accept cash and cards, but not every stall does. Bring some euro notes and coins as a back-up, and use the cash for small snacks and the mug deposit. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Phones & data

Will my UK phone roam for free in Germany?
Not automatically. Since Brexit UK networks are not legally required to offer free EU roaming, so check your plan before you fly, or buy a roaming pass. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Will I get a warning if roaming costs add up?
Ofcom rules in force since 1 October 2024 require UK providers to alert you when you connect abroad and when you approach or pass a spending limit. Still set a limit and switch roaming off for the kids' phones. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Should I get an eSIM or local SIM?
Ofcom lists a local or virtual SIM as a way to cut costs. Compare with your own network's day pass, and use hotel Wi-Fi for maps and downloads. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Safety habits

What number do I ring in an emergency?
112 for fire, ambulance and general emergencies and 110 for police. Both are free from any phone. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Do I have to carry my passport?
There is no legal requirement to carry ID in Germany, but if police ask and you cannot show it, they can escort you to fetch your passport. A photo of it on your phone helps but is not a substitute. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is pickpocketing a worry?
Crime levels are similar to the UK, but gov.uk urges vigilance at airports, stations and crowded places, and not leaving bags unattended. Keep bags zipped in Christmas market crowds. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I check if an area feels right after dark?
Before heading out, look up the area in the Wiley Fox app (www.thewileyfox.com) alongside asking your hotel, then stay on lit main routes. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Local ways

Are shops open on Sundays?
No. In Bavaria shops must close all day on Sundays and holidays, and after 20:00 on weekdays, with exceptions for pharmacies, bakeries and small unmanned mini-markets. Stock up on Saturday. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is the Kaiserburg open when I visit?
Daily 9 to 18 (April to September) and 10 to 16 (October to March), closed 1 Jan, Shrove Tuesday and 24, 25 and 31 December. Last entry is about an hour before closing. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can we see the castle's Deep Well?
Yes, but only on a guided tour, which is included with admission. The Palas audio guide costs 2 euros per person. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas When does the Christmas market run in 2026?
27 November to 24 December, daily 10:00 to 21:00, with the opening ceremony at 17:30 on 27 November. Always double-check on christkindlesmarkt.de. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is the Sister Cities market worth a visit?
Yes. Stalls from Glasgow, Verona, Nice, Atlanta and more are on Rathausplatz, 27 November to 23 December 2026. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is the market OK with pushchairs and dogs?
Dogs are not banned, but crowds make it hard. The cobbles and high stall counters mean access is a bit limited, so allow extra time and avoid peak evenings. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What are the passport rules for UK visitors?
Gov.uk says your passport must be under 10 years old on arrival and valid at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area. You can visit visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180. The gov.uk entry page did not mention ETIAS when we checked. Checked 2026-09 ↗
