Chapter One · The City

Cologne is built for a short break. You step out of the Hauptbahnhof and the cathedral is right in front of you; the Christmas markets, the Altstadt brewhouses, the Rhine and the Chocolate Museum are all within about half an hour's walk. The centre sits on the west bank inside a semicircle of boulevards called the Ringe; across the Hohenzollern Bridge is Deutz, with the classic view back to the cathedral. Creators suggest two to three days, and several said 48 hours felt too short.

At Christmas the city runs several big markets at once, each with its own theme and its own mug: the cathedral market under a giant tree, Heinzels Wintermärchen across two Altstadt squares with an ice rink, the angels' market at Neumarkt, a St Nicholas village at Rudolfplatz and a maritime market by the Chocolate Museum. For families there is a lot to do - a Christmas post office, carousels, Ferris wheels, skating and curling - and most of it is in one walkable loop of about 1.5 km.

For solo travellers Cologne feels friendly and easy, and creators felt safe. The police figures are more mixed: crime is higher than in Munich or Nuremberg, and it is concentrated in the same central squares visitors use, around the cathedral and station, Neumarkt and the Ringe at weekends. The good news is that pickpocketing and street robbery both fell sharply in 2025. This guide shows where to take care, using the latest police report.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Cologne

Cologne is one of the best places in Europe for Christmas markets because so many are close together: the cathedral market, Heinzels Wintermärchen, the Markt der Engel, Nikolausdorf and the harbour market are all walkable, each with its own theme and collectable mug. In 2026 most run from 16 November to 23 December. They are very popular - creators found them packed on weekend evenings - and police send plain-clothes officers after pickpockets. Pickpocketing across Cologne fell by 31% in 2025, but the city centre is still where most of it happens.

The famous one: over 140 red-roofed stalls under a canopy of lights, a giant Christmas tree against the cathedral, and more than 100 free stage events. Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
The cathedral market and Heinzels are shoulder to shoulder on weekend evenings after 5pm. Tuesday or Wednesday at opening time is calmest, and the first week of the season is quieter. Enter the cathedral market from the south side and use the side nooks to get out of the crush.
Via thelittlebasket, Special Spice Travels, Traveling Expats, Travelling Pandas
Pickpockets: fewer, but still here
Pickpocketing across Cologne fell 31% in 2025, but 41% of cases are in the city centre. In December 2024 plain-clothes officers caught several suspected pickpockets at the cathedral market and on the Alter Markt, going for jacket pockets and backpacks. Police advice: valuables split between closed inner pockets, only the cash you need, bags closed and in front.
Via Worthy Travels
Cash, deposits and coins
Carry euros and a card: some stalls are cash only, others card only. Mug deposits were €3-4 in 2025. Market toilets cost about 50 cents to €1 and some need coins; department stores and cafés are the free fallback. Market cash machines charge high fees and can run out at weekends.
Via thelittlebasket, Worthy Travels, Anay And Anayra | Indian Couple In Europe, Adventures of Matt and Nat
Pushchairs and small children
The cathedral market and Heinzels are hard work with a pushchair in the evening, and the Advent market has wood chips underfoot. Go by day with small children. Nikolausdorf and the harbour market are the easiest for families.
Via Mathers On The Map, The Karacsonyi Family, Raising Wildflowers
Have a lost-child plan
Before you go in, agree a meeting point everyone can find - the big Christmas tree at the cathedral market, or the equestrian statue in the middle of the Heumarkt rink. A Wiley Fox QR wristband on each child means anyone who finds them can reach you straight away.
Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln
After the markets close
The markets shut at 21:00-22:00. The walk back through the Altstadt is well lit and busy. On Friday and Saturday nights the Ringe, Zülpicher Straße and Neumarkt get rowdy, and police treat Breslauer Platz and Ebertplatz as hotspots - use the cathedral-side station exit and take a taxi if you're staying out that way.
Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln
New Year's Eve
In 2025/26 bangers were banned inside the Ringe, no fireworks at all were allowed near the cathedral, and the Hohenzollern Bridge and cathedral area closed from 18:30. Police recorded 274 offences in Cologne that night, down from 369. Plans for 2026/27 aren't out yet - check stadt-koeln.de in December.
Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln

In 2026 the cathedral market, Markt der Engel and Nikolausdorf run 16 November to 23 December (closed Sunday 22 November); Heinzels opens a week later on 23 November and its Heumarkt half runs to 10 January 2027; the harbour market runs 13 November to 3 January. Nothing much is open on 24-26 December. Book hotels early - creators found December prices about double, and Düsseldorf is a 30-minute train away if Cologne is full. Buy Chocolate Museum tickets online, and book curling at Heinzels well ahead.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Cologne is a big, busy city with more recorded crime than most German cities its size. Police recorded 11,362 offences per 100,000 residents in 2025 (Cologne and Leverkusen together), down 2.4% on 2024, and in the Eurostat comparison below it sits mid-table among Germany's big cities - well above Munich and Nuremberg, close to Hamburg, Berlin and Düsseldorf. The trend is going the right way: pickpocketing fell by 31% in 2025, street robbery by 21% and robbery overall by 13%. The crime that matters most to visitors is concentrated in the city centre - about 41% of all pickpocketing happens in the central police district that covers the cathedral, the Altstadt, Neumarkt and the Ringe - and police run CCTV at seven hotspots, including the cathedral and station, Breslauer Platz, Ebertplatz and Neumarkt. This rating is for the city as a whole, not street by street: German police do not publish street-level crime data.

The creators

Creators found Cologne relaxed, friendly and easy to walk. A family with a baby and a toddler found it very clean and safe (Where Is Briggs), a senior solo traveller felt safe in the historic and tourist areas (SeniorSoloTravel), and a solo woman ate alone on a Monday night and walked back to her hostel without a second thought (Rachel Wells). NiteshOnTheGo felt noticeably safer than in Paris. Nobody reported being pickpocketed. The recurring complaint is crowds: the cathedral market and Heinzels are shoulder to shoulder on weekend evenings, and Schildergasse was too crowded to move on a December Saturday.

What the official figures say

Figures are from the Cologne police's PKS 2025 annual report, which covers Cologne and Leverkusen together, so the headline rate is for both cities. No street-level data is published; the smallest unit is the police district, and the central district (PI 1: Altstadt-Nord, Altstadt-Süd, Neustadt-Nord, Neustadt-Süd) recorded 32,100 offences in 2025, down 2%. In that district street crime fell 16% and pickpocketing 37%, but violent crime rose 7.5% (1,785 cases) and assaults 5%. Across the police area, sexual offences rose 7% and recorded rapes 21% (417). Rates are per resident, so the millions of commuters, shoppers and tourists in the centre push them up. Police CCTV locations are the ones police themselves describe as crime hotspots with a wide range of offences.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Cathedral forecourt (Domplatte) & Hauptbahnhof
Take most care: Evenings, weekends and market season

Police list the cathedral and station area as a crime hotspot with a wide range of offences, and it has police CCTV. It is also where most visitors arrive, so it is packed with people and luggage - ideal conditions for pickpockets. The whole city-centre district recorded 2,276 pickpocketing cases in 2025, 41% of the city total, although that was down 37%. Keep phones in inner pockets, bags zipped in front, and don't leave luggage unattended while you check a map.

Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln
Breslauer Platz (behind the station) & Ebertplatz
Take most care: Late evening and night

Both are on the police list of crime hotspots with CCTV. Breslauer Platz is the station's back entrance and long-distance bus stop; Ebertplatz is a sunken square on the northern Ringe about 15 minutes' walk from the cathedral. Neither is somewhere travellers need to linger - use the cathedral-side exit of the station, and take a taxi or the tram rather than cutting through at night.

Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln
The Ringe & Zülpicher Straße
Take most care: Friday and Saturday nights, and 11 November

Cologne's nightlife strip. The Ringe are a police CCTV hotspot, and the Ringe and Zülpicher Straße are weapon ban zones from 20:00 to 06:00 at weekends, before public holidays and on carnival days: knives of any blade length are banned. On 11 November, the start of carnival, tens of thousands party here and the city fences off the Zülpicher area. Fine to walk through early in the evening; late at night, get a taxi back.

Via Max and Sidney
Neumarkt
Take most care: Evenings

A big transport hub and the home of the Markt der Engel. Police include Neumarkt in their CCTV crime hotspots, and creators found the angels' market very crowded after 5pm. Enjoy the market, but keep valuables close, agree a meeting point, and don't wait around the underground station entrances late at night.

Via Travelling Pandas, Traveling Expats
Cathedral market & Heinzels in the evening
Take most care: Weekend evenings after 5pm

Creators describe the cathedral market as 'could barely move' at night and Heinzels as 'Disneyland busy' after dark - hard going with a pushchair or small children. The little Advent market next to the cathedral market has wood chips underfoot, which are hard for pushchairs. Go at opening time or on a weekday afternoon.

Via Travelling Pandas, Brickey Vlog, The Karacsonyi Family, Mathers On The Map, thelittlebasket
Schildergasse & Hohe Straße
Take most care: Saturdays in December

The two main shopping streets link the cathedral with Neumarkt. Creators quote 13,000-15,000 shoppers an hour, and one found Schildergasse too crowded to move on a December Saturday. Crowds like this are where pickpockets work - police warn of thieves who bump into you, spill something on you or ask for directions while an accomplice steals.

Via Eren Sayar, Prowalk Tours, Travel Ranked, Island Hopper TV

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

The bump, the spill and the map
Police say pickpockets on Christmas markets work in pairs: one jostles you, "accidentally" spills something on your coat or asks for directions, while the other takes your wallet or phone. In December 2024 Cologne police arrested several suspected pickpockets at the cathedral market and on the Alter Markt, trying jacket pockets and backpacks.
Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln
Lost your card?
Ring the German card-blocking line on +49 116 116, or your bank's own number, straight away, then report the theft to police. Keep cards, cash and your ID in different closed inner pockets, as police advise.
Source: Polizeipräsidium Köln / Stadt Köln
Deposits on everything
Not a scam, but it catches people out: Glühwein comes with a mug deposit (€3-4 in 2025), and each market has its own mug. Return it at a drinks stall of the same market for your money, or keep it as a souvenir.
Via Travelling Pandas, Adventures of Matt and Nat, Seeking Paradise Bugs
Expensive speciality food
Pascha TV spent about €70 on six speciality dishes at the Heumarkt and says most weren't worth it. The classics - Reibekuchen, bratwurst, Handbrot - are better value.
Via Pascha TV
Chapter Four · Where to base yourself
Neighbourhoods

Where to stay

Pick an area first, then a hotel. The old town is compact, so most bases are within a short walk of the markets.

Around the cathedral & Hauptbahnhof
First-timers · Christmas markets · Arriving by train

Unbeatable for a short trip: the station, the cathedral market and the Altstadt are on your doorstep, and trains to Düsseldorf, Bonn and Aachen leave from here. Creators stayed at the Eden Hotel beside the market (handy for breaks with children), Urban Loft and the Marriott. The catch: police list the cathedral and station area as a crime hotspot with CCTV, and it is very busy late at night, so pick a hotel on the cathedral side rather than behind the station at Breslauer Platz.

Via The Karacsonyi Family, Worthy Travels, thelittlebasket, Wiki Peaks
Altstadt (Alter Markt & Heumarkt)
Christmas markets · Brewhouses · Couples

The old town between the cathedral and the Rhine: cobbled squares, the brewhouses and Heinzels Wintermärchen right outside. She Run the World's budget hotel overlooked one of the main markets and she calls the Altstadt the most central base for all of them. Expect noise from bars and market crowds until late, and busy streets at weekends.

Via She Run the World, Walshy’s Travels, City Zen
Deutz (east bank)
Families · Views · Quieter nights

Across the Hohenzollern Bridge, a few minutes' walk or one stop from the cathedral. The Hyatt Regency's best rooms look straight across at the cathedral, and Hylo Abroad's boys loved its pool. Quieter at night than the Altstadt, and next to the KölnTriangle viewpoint. Check the trade-fair calendar: big Messe events push prices up.

Via Hylo Abroad | Tanya Rasmussen, Wiki Peaks
Belgian Quarter (Belgisches Viertel)
Solo travellers · Food & cafés

Stylish streets of boutiques, espresso bars and galleries just inside the Ringe, a short walk from Nikolausdorf, Heavenue and the Stadtgarten market. City Zen warns of noise until about 2am. It borders the Ringe and Zülpicher Straße nightlife strip, which is a weapon ban zone on weekend nights, so choose a quiet side street.

Via City Zen, Alice & Lucas Travel, Traveller James
Südstadt & Rheinauhafen
Families · Longer stays

Leafy and residential, popular with young families, with bakeries, bookshops and the Rhine promenade. You're close to the harbour market and the Chocolate Museum and about 20-30 minutes' walk from the cathedral, or a short tram ride. Walshy's Travels liked the supervised adventure playground in Friedenspark.

Via City Zen, Walshy’s Travels
Budget: stay in Düsseldorf
Budget · Two cities

Travelling Pandas found Cologne hotels and flights about double the price in December, so they based themselves in Düsseldorf - about 30 minutes by train, €26 return in 2025 - and did both cities' markets. Worth pricing if Cologne is full.

Via Travelling Pandas, Max and Sidney

Quick tips

Sights by day, markets by night
In December it's dark by about 4:30pm. Do the cathedral, bridge and museums first and save the markets for when the lights come on - but not too late on a weekend.
Go early in the season, and midweek
Creators who went in the first week, or on a Tuesday or Wednesday at opening time, had far more space. Weekend evenings are shoulder to shoulder.
Wear grippy shoes
Cobbles everywhere and wet December pavements. Comfortable, waterproof shoes matter more than smart ones, and a pushchair with big wheels helps.
Two or three days
One day covers the cathedral and a couple of markets; two or three lets you see them all without rushing and add the Chocolate Museum or a day trip.
Plan for Christmas itself
Most markets close on 23 December, and shops close on 25, 26 and 27 December and 1 January. Heinzels on the Heumarkt and the harbour market stay open into January, but Heinzels rests on 24 and 25 December.
Leave the car - and the penknife
Car parks fill fast on market weekends, and knives are banned in the Ringe and Zülpicher Straße zones on weekend nights. The train drops you right at the cathedral.
