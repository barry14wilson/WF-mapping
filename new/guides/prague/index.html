Chapter One · The City

Prague's historic centre straddles the Vltava. On the east bank are the Old Town (Staré Město), with the Old Town Square and the Astronomical Clock, the Jewish Quarter (Josefov) and the New Town (Nové Město) around Wenceslas Square; Charles Bridge crosses to the Lesser Town (Malá Strana) and up the hill to Prague Castle. The centre is compact and walkable but cobbled and hilly on the castle side, and trams and three metro lines cover the rest. Creators suggest three or four days.

At Christmas the two big markets on the Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square run from the last Saturday of November into January, and the Old Town tree lights up to music every hour after dark. Smaller, cheaper markets fill local squares - Náměstí Míru in Vinohrady is the locals' favourite. A lamplighter lights the gas lamps on Charles Bridge in the weeks before Christmas, and there are rinks, concerts and a free children's programme at the main market.

For solo travellers Prague feels safe and friendly, and English is widely spoken. Plan for packed streets in December (especially weekends), dark afternoons from about 4pm, icy cobbles, and the money traps: pay by card in koruna, never change money on the street, and use a ride app instead of a street taxi. Large shops close at noon on 24 December and all day on 25 and 26 December.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Prague

Prague's two big Christmas markets, on the Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square, open on Saturday 28 November 2026 and run to 6 January 2027. They are pretty, food-heavy and very crowded, and both are run by the same company. The smaller markets in local squares - Náměstí Míru, Republic Square, Tylovo náměstí - are cheaper, more local and mostly close on 24 December. Prague also does the Christmas traditions well: a lamplighter on Charles Bridge, carol singers on church steps and fried carp on Christmas Eve.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
December is Prague's busiest month - over 600,000 visitors, creators say. The Old Town market fills up towards the weekend; weeknights and daytimes are calmer, and the smaller squares stay manageable all month.
Via Prowalk Tours, Here's Good, Postcard And A Pint, Dave Mani
Police and barriers
Police step up uniformed and armed patrols at the markets every Advent, and concrete barriers protect the Old Town, Wenceslas Square and Republic Square markets (2024, latest year published). The UK Foreign Office says terrorist attacks in Czechia can't be ruled out.
Source: Czech police / official sources
Pickpockets
Watch bags in the queues and on the viewing walkway, at the clock on the hour, and on tram 22. Don't leave phones on café tables. Report a theft at a police station within 24 hours for insurance - the one at Jungmannovo náměstí 9 is open 24 hours with interpreters.
Via Real Prague Guides, HONEST GUIDE, Wolters World
Prices and payment
Mulled wine was about CZK 100 at the big markets and CZK 60-80 at the smaller ones (2024-25); sweet waffles about CZK 120-180. The big markets take cards; Náměstí Míru stalls were cash only. Food is often priced per 100 g.
Via Here's Good, The Traveling ZAM, Real Prague Guides, Tohl Time

Agree a meeting point everyone can find - the tree on the Old Town Square or the statue of St Wenceslas at the top of the square. A Wiley Fox QR wristband on each child means anyone who finds them can reach you straight away.

Cold, dark and icy
Highs around 4°C, nights below freezing, dark by 4:30pm, and slippery cobbles. Waterproof boots with grip, gloves and a hat; plan sights for the morning.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Prague is a calm city for visitors: creators - solo women and families included - felt safe day and night, and local guides say there are no no-go areas. The risks are pickpocketing in crowds and losing money to traps rather than to crime. Czech police registered 34,853 crimes in Prague in 2025, down 9% on 2024: 2,492 per 100,000 residents, about 1.6 times the Czech average, which the statistics office puts down partly to tourists and commuters being counted as victims but not as residents. Violent crime (115 per 100,000) is level with the regions around Brno and Plzeň and below Ostrava's. Pickpocketing makes up about 9% of Prague's property crime - a far bigger share than elsewhere in the country - and the UK Foreign Office warns of it on airport transport, the trams to the castle and in busy tourist areas. The other danger creators stress is the trams: heavy, fast and with right of way. This rating is for the city as a whole: Czechia does not publish street-level crime data.

The creators

Creators repeatedly call Prague one of the safest cities in Europe, and several solo women walked home late at night without worry. Local guides say genuine scams are now rare and the topic is overblown; what does happen is street money-changers handing over worthless notes, exchange offices with terrible rates, overpriced street taxis, food sold by weight and restaurants that add a service charge. In December the Old Town, Charles Bridge and the castle are packed, especially at weekends. A few places get rowdy after dark - the bottom of Wenceslas Square, the park by the main station, Dlouhá Street and around Anděl.

What the official figures say

Figures are from the Czech Statistical Office's crime publication (June 2026), which republishes Czech Police data for 2025, cross-checked against the police's own regional table. Prague is both a city and a region, so these are city figures. Rates are the statistics office's own, per 100,000 residents at mid-year. 2025 in Prague: all registered crime 34,853 (-9.3%), property crime 23,270 (-9.4%), violent crime 1,609 (+0.8%), robbery 204 (+12.7%), home burglary 724 (-1.0%). Czech law counts many small thefts as minor offences rather than crimes, and the police clear only 27% of Prague cases (the lowest of 14 regions, because most are anonymous property crimes), so compare Prague only with other Czech regions, not with other countries. Czechia publishes no street-level data, so this guide's map shows the markets and sights and the places creators and police name. On New Year's Eve 2025/26 Prague 1 reported no serious incidents; police logged 269 reports in the district, 54 of them about fireworks.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Astronomical Clock, Old Town Square and Charles Bridge
Take most care: December afternoons, evenings and weekends

The crowd hotspots, and where creators and the UK Foreign Office warn of pickpockets - also on tram 22 to the castle and on airport transport. Most phones and wallets go when they are left on café and restaurant tables. Keep bags zipped and in front, and agree a meeting point with children.

Via Real Prague Guides, Wolters World, Destination Well Known, HONEST GUIDE
Trams and road crossings
Take most care: Always

Trams have right of way, often don't stop at crossings without lights and need several metres to brake. Local guides say this is the real physical danger in Prague. Hold children's hands at tram tracks, and don't dash across the six-lane road at the main station.

Via HONEST GUIDE, Real Prague Guides
Wenceslas Square, Dlouhá Street and Anděl at night
Take most care: Late evenings and weekends

Clubs, drunk crowds and touts at the bottom of Wenceslas Square, where a local guide links some bars to overcharging and, rarely, spiked drinks; rowdy bars around Dlouhá; and crowds around Anděl station. Don't follow touts, check prices, and use an app taxi home.

Via Real Prague Guides, Destination Well Known
The park by the main station (Vrchlického sady)
Take most care: After dark

Creators say phones and wallets go missing here after dark, though it is cleaner than it was. Use the station's main entrances and the lifts rather than cutting through.

Via Real Prague Guides
Ticket inspections
Take most care: Always

No barriers, but plain-clothes inspectors check often, especially at the Petřín funicular. An unvalidated ticket counts as none: the fine is CZK 1,200 on the spot or CZK 1,500-2,000 later (2026). Activate app tickets before you board - there's no signal underground.

Via Real Prague Guides, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), Nash & Tereza, Carol & Max, HONEST GUIDE

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Street money-changers
People outside exchange offices, by cash machines, at ticket machines (some in orange vests) and even at the airport bus stop offer a 'good rate' and hand over worthless foreign or old notes. Never change money on the street - pay by card.
Via HONEST GUIDE, Real Prague Guides, Destination Well Known
'0% commission' exchange offices
Commission is banned by law, so the sign means nothing: the cost is in the rate. Creators saw 17 to 25 koruna per euro on the same street. You can cancel an exchange within 3 hours at the same office (Czech National Bank).
Via HONEST GUIDE, Real Prague Guides, Wolters World
Cash machines and card terminals
Avoid Euronet and other independent cash machines in the Old Town, and always choose koruna when a machine offers your home currency - one creator saw a €175 fee on a withdrawal.
Via HONEST GUIDE, Carol & Max, Wolters World
Street taxis
Taxis waiting at the main station, the airport rank and in the Old Town have overcharged - one hidden-camera ride cost 750 CZK against 231. Use Bolt, Uber or Liftago for an upfront price. The airport's own fare guide is about CZK 800 to the centre.
Via HONEST GUIDE, Toughest, Nash & Tereza, Real Prague Guides, Carol & Max
Food sold by weight
Prague ham and other market dishes are priced per 100 g and weighed after cutting - 300 g came to 387 CZK. Ask for a weight (say 150 g) and the total before they cut.
Via Real Prague Guides, Never Two Full, Nate and Katrina Do the World
Tourist-trap restaurants
Tented restaurants on the Old Town Square and some on Wenceslas Square charge about four times local prices, add 12-15% service, serve a 'large beer' as a litre, and charge for bread. Check the menu and the bill; a daily lunch menu a street away costs half.
Via Nash & Tereza, HONEST GUIDE, Wolters World, Real Prague Guides
Fake police
The UK Foreign Office warns of fake plain-clothes police asking to see your money and passport. Real officers can't demand to see your cash - offer to go to a police station or call 112.
Source: Czech police / official sources
Chapter Four · Where to base yourself
Neighbourhoods

Where to stay

Pick an area first, then a hotel. The old town is compact, so most bases are within a short walk of the markets.

Staré Město (Old Town)
First-timers · Christmas markets · Walking

Everything is on the doorstep, and creators suggest it for winter because daylight is short and the hilly Lesser Town gets slippery. It is the priciest and noisiest area: pick a calm street or a courtyard room, and avoid Karlova, Kaprova and the party street Dlouhá. Mid-range hotels from about $80-150 a night outside peak dates (2026).

Via Real Prague Guides, Nash & Tereza, Destination Well Known, The Ultimate Travel Guide, CORR Travel Europe Your Way
Malá Strana (Lesser Town)
Couples · Families · Quiet nights

Baroque streets under the castle, over Charles Bridge. Very quiet at night - embassies, no clubs - and a family with a toddler loved it in January. The catch is hills and cobbles, which are hard with a pushchair or in snow; choose a hotel near a tram stop.

Via Destination Well Known, Real Prague Guides, Nash & Tereza, Walshy's Travels, ViaTravelers
Nové Město (New Town)
Families · Good value · Transport

The best mid-range choice, 20-30% cheaper than the Old Town and a short walk from it, with metro and trams everywhere: about $55-100 a night (2026). Quieter pockets are around Charles Square, the Dancing House and the Powder Tower; avoid the hotels on Wenceslas Square itself, which stay loud late.

Via The Ultimate Travel Guide, MultiCityTrips, Destination Well Known, Real Prague Guides, Nash & Tereza
Vinohrady
Families · Solo travellers · Local life

Leafy, residential and a few metro stops from the centre, with the locals' Christmas markets at Náměstí Míru and Jiřího z Poděbrad, parks and good cafés. Mostly apartments and guesthouses, about $45-120 a night (2025-26).

Via The Traveler’s Atlas, ViaTravelers, Exotic Vacation, The Ultimate Travel Guide, Carol & Max
Holešovice
Families · Budget · Museums

A former industrial district five to ten minutes from the centre by tram, with Letná and Stromovka parks and the National Technical Museum for children. International trains from Berlin stop here.

Via Nash & Tereza, MultiCityTrips, ViaTravelers, Real Prague Guides
Dejvice (Prague 6)
Quiet nights · Families · Early flights

Residential, calm and cheaper, on metro line A and handy for the airport bus. If you stay further out, creators say pick somewhere on the metro rather than relying on buses.

Via Nash & Tereza, Real Prague Guides

There's no metro to the airport. Quick tips

Pay by card, in koruna
Cards work almost everywhere. Always choose koruna when asked, avoid Euronet machines, and never change money on the street. Keep a few coins for toilets and the smaller markets, some of which are cash only.
Go early, and on weekdays
December is high season. Charles Bridge, the clock and the castle are calm at 7-8am and packed by mid-morning; the Old Town market is easier on a weeknight than at the weekend.
Use an app taxi
Bolt, Uber or Liftago show the price up front. Avoid street taxis at the main station, the airport rank and in the Old Town.
Ask the weight before they cut
Ham and other market food is sold per 100 g. Ask for a weight and the total, and check restaurant bills for service charges and extras.
Book dinners and concerts
Restaurants fill in December - book three or four days ahead - and The Nutcracker and other Christmas shows sell out months in advance. Buy from the venue's own site.
24 to 26 December
Large shops close at noon on 24 December and all day on 25 and 26 December, and many restaurants and sights close too; the castle buildings are shut on the 24th. The Old Town and Wenceslas Square markets stay open with shorter hours. Stock up by the 23rd if you're in an apartment.

Paying for transport

What do the main Prague public transport tickets cost in 2026?
From 1 January 2026 a full-fare ticket for the whole of Prague costs: 30 minutes CZK 36 (app) or CZK 39 (paper); 90 minutes CZK 46 or CZK 50; 24 hours CZK 140 or CZK 150; 72 hours CZK 340 or CZK 350. The app price is the cheaper one. One ticket covers metro, tram, bus and the other PID services in Prague. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Do my children need tickets in Prague?
Children under 6 travel free across the whole PID system if accompanied by someone over 10. Children aged 6 to 10 travel free in Prague, and children aged 10 to 15 travel free in Prague if they can prove their age (a document with name, date of birth and photo). Outside Prague, ages 6 to 15 pay half fare. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Do grandparents get free or cheaper travel?
Anyone aged 65 or over travels free on Prague metro, trams, buses and ferries, and only needs to show proof of age if inspected (passport or national ID). Ages 60 to 65 pay half price (a 24-hour ticket is CZK 75 on the Prague transport company's page). Checked 2026-10 ↗
Is a 24-hour or 72-hour pass worth it for a family?
Tickets are per person, but under-15s travel free in Prague, so a family of two adults and children needs only two adult tickets. Two adults on a 24-hour ticket cost CZK 280 (app, 2026). Single 90-minute tickets (CZK 46 each) are cheaper if you only make two or three trips. Checked 2026-10 ↗
What is the PID Lítačka app and does it work in English?
It is the official app for tickets, journey planning and closure alerts. It is available in English, takes cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, and lets you buy now and activate later, or set a future start time. You need a data connection to activate a ticket, but not afterwards. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Can I tap my bank card on board?
PID says card payment in vehicles is an extra service, not guaranteed. If the terminal fails and you cannot pay another way, the driver may refuse to carry you, so have the Lítačka app or cash as a back-up. Whether you can tap in at metro stations or ticket machines is not yet confirmed. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Do I have to stamp my ticket?
Yes for paper tickets: validate once at the first boarding (at the yellow validators in metro stations or at the airport platforms). App tickets must be activated before you board; the default countdown is 2 minutes. An unvalidated ticket counts as no ticket. Checked 2026-10 ↗
What is the fine for travelling without a valid ticket?
In Prague the fine is CZK 1,200 on the spot, CZK 1,500 if paid within 15 days, or CZK 2,000 otherwise (2026). It drops to CZK 50 if you show a valid ticket or discount proof within 15 days. Checked 2026-10 ↗
How do I get from the airport on public transport?
Buy a 90-minute ticket (CZK 46 app or CZK 50 paper, 2026) before boarding. Take trolleybus 59 to Veleslavín (about 15 minutes) then metro line A, or bus 100 to Zličín (about 18 minutes) then metro line B. Night buses 907 and 910 take about 45 minutes to the centre. Checked 2026-10 ↗
What is the Airport Express and is it good value?
It is a bus from the airport to the Main Railway Station (about 40 minutes). A one-way ticket is CZK 200 (airport website, read 14 Oct 2026). Normal PID tickets are not valid on it. Buy from the Visitor Centre in arrivals or from the driver. For most families, 59 plus the metro is far cheaper. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Which taxi or ride app should I use from the airport?
Use an app that shows the total fare before you confirm. The airport says Prague's taxi fare cap ended on 1 October 2026, so prices can now differ between firms. Its own figure for a typical trip to the centre (about 20 km) is around CZK 800. Uber Airport shows a fixed fare and is bound by limits agreed with the airport. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Can I use the metro with a pushchair or wheelchair?
The transport company says lifts serve 45 stations, though another line on the same page says 48 of 61, so the count is unclear. Lifts can break down; check the live lift status page before you go. Stations without lifts are hard to use with a pushchair. Checked 2026-10 ↗

Money & tipping

What currency do I use, and can I pay in euros?
Prague uses the Czech koruna (CZK). The Czech National Bank published EUR 1 = CZK 24.375 for 13 October 2026. Shops are not required to take euros; some tourist places do at poor rates. Pay in koruna wherever you can. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Where is it safe to change money?
Change money only at a designated bureau de change, never from people on the street. The Czech National Bank also says rates vary a lot between offices, so compare the buy and sell rates before you go in. Checked 2026-10 ↗
What is the 'no commission' or '0%' trap?
Czech law bans commission on currency exchange, so '0% commission' means nothing. The real cost is hidden in the gap between buy and sell rates. The central bank says a gap of several koruna is a reason not to proceed. A tourist-centre kiosk can leave you well short of the fair rate. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Can I change my mind after exchanging money?
Yes, within 3 hours at the same bureau (the clock pauses outside its opening hours). You are entitled to a written proof of cancellation in at least Czech and English. For amounts over EUR 1,000 you can cancel only the first EUR 1,000. Machines allow 3 working days. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Should I pay in koruna or pounds on a card machine or cash machine?
Choose koruna. If a cash machine or card terminal offers to charge you in pounds, that is dynamic currency conversion and it usually gives a worse rate. A 2026 local guide specifically warns about this at airport cash machines. Checked 2026-10 ↗
Christmas Will cards be accepted?
Prague Zoo says all its ticket offices take payment cards, and some Prague City Tourism towers take card only. Card acceptance at individual Christmas market stalls is not yet confirmed, so carry some koruna for stalls and toilets. Checked 2026-10 ↗
How much should I tip?
Tipping is customary rather than required. About 10 percent for good service, or rounding up the bill, is typical. Check whether a service charge is already on the bill first. This comes from private guides, not an official source. Checked 2026-10 ↗
