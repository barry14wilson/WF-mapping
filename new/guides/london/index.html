Chapter One · The City

London is huge - more a region than a city - so the single best decision you make is where you stay. Creators agree on one rule above all others: book within a ten-minute walk of a Tube station, and group each day by neighbourhood rather than criss-crossing town.

For families it is one of the easiest capitals in the world. The big museums are free, the parks are enormous, and the Tube, buses and river boats reach almost everything. Children under 11 travel free on the Tube and buses with a paying adult.

For solo travellers it is lively every night of the week. The risks are ordinary big-city ones, and they cluster in predictable places. This guide shows you where, using the same police data that powers the Wiley Fox map.

Chapter Two · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

London is a well-policed city and most visits pass without trouble. The main risk for visitors is theft: pickpocketing and phone snatching in the busiest central areas. Violence against tourists is rare and concentrated late at night around nightlife and some outer town centres.

The creators

Every creator we reviewed describes London as comfortable for visitors, including families. The repeated warnings are practical ones: watch your phone near the kerb, keep bags zipped in crowds, never leave a phone on a pub table, and look right before you cross.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Leicester Square, Soho, Covent Garden & Oxford Street
Take most care: All day, busiest evenings and weekends

The highest recorded crime in our sample, and nearly half of it is theft. Phone snatching by riders on e-bikes is the number-one warning from creators. Pedicab (rickshaw) drivers are unregulated - agree the price before you get in, or better, don't.

4,587 crimes a month nearby · top: other theft, theft from the person, violent crime
Via Love and London, Evan Edinger, Wolters World, Sophie Nadeau, Suitcase Monkey
Westminster Bridge & the South Bank by the London Eye
Take most care: Daytime crowds

Prime pickpocket territory and the spot for the shell-and-cup street game. You will never win it - the 'winners' are part of the act.

3,266 crimes a month nearby · top: theft from the person, other theft, violent crime
Via Love and London, Wolters World
Whitechapel
Take most care: Late evening, around the station and main road

Brick Lane and the markets are worth a daytime visit. Recorded crime is lower than the West End, but violence and anti-social behaviour make up a larger share. Some creators suggest it as a budget base; with children or a late arrival, we'd stay further west.

2,435 crimes a month nearby · top: violent crime, anti social behaviour, shoplifting
Via The Tour Guy, Love and London
Croydon town centre
Take most care: Evenings, around West Croydon

Violence is the top recorded category here. Cheap 'central London' hotel deals are often in Croydon - a long commute and not where we'd base a family trip.

1,354 crimes a month nearby · top: violent crime, anti social behaviour, shoplifting
Via Destination Well Known
Lewisham town centre
Take most care: Evenings

Not a visitor area. A third of recorded crime is violence or robbery, the highest share in our sample. There's no reason to base a trip here.

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Phone snatching
Riders on e-bikes grab phones from people walking near the kerb. Step into a shop doorway to check maps, and keep your phone on the building side.
Via Sophie Nadeau, Happy to Wander, Destination Well Known, Suitcase Monkey, Dutch Travel Maniac
Pub table theft
Phones and wallets left on tables vanish in seconds. Keep bags in front of you or under the table, never on the back of your chair.
Via Wolters World, Happy to Wander
Pedicab overcharging
Rickshaws in the West End have charged tourists hundreds of pounds for short rides. There is no regulated fare.
Via Evan Edinger, Sophie Nadeau
Street games & 'free' gifts
The shell game, a 'found' ring or a free bracelet always ends with someone wanting money.
Via Wolters World, Love and London
Fake petitions & charity collectors
Clipboard petitions around the sights are often a set-up for a donation or a distraction. A polite 'no thanks' and keep walking.
Via Wolters World
Currency conversion at ATMs and card machines
Always choose to pay in pounds. Letting the machine convert to your home currency can cost far more.
Via Wolters World, Love and London
Unofficial theatre tickets
Buy from the theatre box office, TodayTix or the official TKTS booth in Leicester Square - not from people on the street.
Via Wolters World, Happy to Wander

Chapter Six · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around London

From the airport
Heathrow is on the Piccadilly and Elizabeth lines - tap your card and go. Creators rate the Heathrow Express as quick but poor value unless you are staying at Paddington. Check late-night options before booking a cheap arrival into Stansted or Luton; the last trains can leave you with an expensive taxi.
Via Happy to Wander, Love and London, Wolters World, Evan Edinger
Pay with contactless
Tap the same card or phone in and out. Daily caps are applied automatically: £8.90 a day for zones 1-2 on Tube and rail, frozen until March 2027. Paper tickets and paper day Travelcards cost more.
Via Love and London, Evan Edinger, The Tour Guy
Buses
£1.75 a ride with unlimited changes within an hour (the Hopper fare). Routes 11 and 24 pass many of the big sights and make a cheap sightseeing tour from the top deck.
Via Love and London, Sophie Nadeau, Happy to Wander
Children
Under-11s travel free on the Tube and buses with a paying adult. Avoid the Tube at 7:30-9am and 5-7pm with small children and pushchairs.
Via Happy to Wander
Taxis
Black cabs are licensed and heavily regulated - if the orange light is on, it's free. Free Now books licensed cabs by app and shows the fare estimate. Ignore unlicensed minicabs that approach you in the street.
Via Love and London, Happy to Wander, Wolters World
By river
The Uber Boat (Thames Clippers) is part of the public network - tap in and ride to Greenwich or the Tower. A relaxing option when little legs are tired.
Via Destination Well Known, Wolters World
Walk more
Many central Tube stops are only 5-15 minutes apart on foot. Victoria to Buckingham Palace, for example, is faster walking. Stand on the right on escalators.
Via Wolters World, Evan Edinger
Late at night
If you're on your own late, a licensed cab is worth the money over a long walk. Plan the route home before you head out - Wiley Fox's Safest Route helps.
Via Love and London

Quick tips

Look right
Traffic drives on the left. Wolters World has seen tourists hit stepping out the wrong way - follow the 'look right' markings.
Book the free stuff
Sky Garden, Horizon 22 and museum time slots are free but go fast. Book as soon as your dates are set.
Don't overload days
One or two paid sights a day, grouped by area. London rewards wandering.
Skip the 'awful four' combo
Love and London's name for pricey ticket bundles (Dungeon, Shrek, Aquarium, Madame Tussauds). Fine for small kids; poor value otherwise.
Keep kids out of phone boxes
The famous red boxes are often used as toilets. Photo outside, not in.

Chapter Seven · Local tips
The small things visitors worry about

Local tips for London

Every tip here was checked against an official or trusted source (TfL, National Rail, gov.uk, NHS, the Royal Parks and others) on the date shown. Rules and prices change, so tap the link to double-check before you travel.

Paying for transport

Can I pay for the Tube with my phone?
Yes – just tap Apple Pay, Google Pay or a contactless card on the yellow reader; no need to buy a ticket or Oyster card first. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Will I overpay if I travel a lot in one day?
No – pay as you go is capped daily and across the Monday–Sunday week, but only if you touch in and out with the same card or phone every time. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Why was I charged twice or the wrong fare?
Keep your other cards away from your phone when you tap – the reader can pick the wrong card ("card clash"), which breaks your cap. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Do I touch out on the bus?
No – on buses and trams you only touch in when you board. On the Tube and trains, always touch out too or you may be charged a maximum fare. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Will my overseas card cost extra on the Tube?
It might – TfL warns non-UK cards can carry overseas transaction fees, so use a fee-free travel card or check with your bank before you go. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Do my kids need a ticket on the Tube?
Up to four children under 11 travel free with a fare-paying adult on the Tube, DLR, Overground and Elizabeth line, and they're free on buses and trams too. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is there a cheaper fare for my teenager?
Yes – 11–15-year-olds pay child rates; visitors can ask station staff to add the Young Visitor discount (half adult pay as you go, up to 14 days) to a Visitor Oyster card. From 16 it's adult fares. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do families save on UK train tickets?
A Family & Friends Railcard gives adults 1/3 off and kids 5–15 up to 60% off – at least one named adult and one child must travel together, and it works as a digital card in the app. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is there a railcard for two adults travelling together?
The Two Together Railcard saves 1/3 on train fares for two named adults travelling together – worth it for couples or friends doing day trips out of London. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can the kids ride a Santander hire bike?
Only an adult 18+ can hire, but they can authorise riders aged 14 and over under their hire – younger children can't ride them. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I avoid stairs with a buggy or wheelchair?
Check the step-free symbols on the official Tube map before you set off – many central stations still have stairs only. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Water & snacks

Where do I get cheap water and snacks?
Skip the coffee shop and pop into a Tesco, Sainsbury's or Boots – their lunch meal deals (main, snack and drink for one price) are the best-value quick lunch in London. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can I ask for free tap water in a pub or restaurant?
Yes – any premises licensed to serve alcohol on site must give free tap water on request where it's reasonably available, so just ask. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Where can I refill my water bottle for free?
Download the free Refill app – it maps cafés, shops and fountains that will top up your bottle at no charge. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Toilets

Where's the nearest toilet?
Use the free Great British Public Toilet Map (toiletmap.org.uk) – it shows the nearest public loos, including accessible and baby-change facilities. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Do I have to pay for toilets at stations?
No – toilets at London's National Rail stations are free, and TfL publishes a map of which Tube and rail stations have them. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can I use a toilet without buying anything?
Museums and galleries usually have free loos, and in the City of London the Community Toilet Scheme lets you use toilets in participating shops, pubs and restaurants without buying. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Parks & play

Are London's playgrounds free?
Yes – the Royal Parks run 14 playgrounds, including Greenwich Park, Gloucester Gate in Regent's Park and Bushy Park, and they're free to use. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can we get into the Diana Memorial Playground?
It's free for children up to 12 with a supervising adult, but it fills up fast on warm days – arrive at opening (10am) to avoid queues. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Where can we go on a rainy day without spending much?
The big museums – British Museum, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, V&A, National Gallery and Tate Modern – are free; book a free timed ticket online and note special exhibitions cost extra. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Money & tipping

Should I tip if there's a service charge on the bill?
No need to add more – a service charge is effectively the tip, and by law all tips and service charges must be passed on in full to staff. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Does a tip paid on card reach the staff?
Yes – since October 2024 employers must pass on 100% of tips, including card tips, to workers without deductions. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Phones & data

Will using my phone data in the UK cost a fortune?
Check your home network's roaming charges before you arrive and switch off data roaming if unsure – a local SIM or travel eSIM plus free Wi-Fi is often much cheaper. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What should I note down in case my phone is stolen?
Dial *#06# to get your phone's IMEI number and write it down – police and your network use it to block and trace a stolen handset. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Safety habits

How do I stop my phone being snatched?
Step back from the kerb and stand near a wall when using your phone, keep calls short and don't text while walking – thieves on bikes and mopeds target distracted people. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What if someone grabs my bag or phone?
Let it go – the Met's advice is never to confront a thief; your safety matters more than the device. Set up a screen lock and remote wipe before your trip. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Which areas should I be extra alert in?
Before heading out, check the neighbourhood's safety score in the Wiley Fox app (www.thewileyfox.com) and pair it with the Met's phone-safety habits. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Which number do I call for help?
Call 999 in an emergency (112 also works from mobiles). For urgent but non-emergency medical help, use NHS 111 by phone or online at 111.nhs.uk – it's available 24/7. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Local ways

Which side of the escalator do I stand on?
Stand on the right and leave the left clear for people walking – it's one of the quickest ways to blend in on the Tube. Checked 2026-09 ↗
