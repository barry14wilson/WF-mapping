Chapter One · The City

Munich is a big city with a compact, walkable centre. From the Hauptbahnhof it's a 15-minute walk through the pedestrian zone past Karlsplatz (Stachus) to Marienplatz, the main square, with the Frauenkirche, the Viktualienmarkt food market and the Residenz palace all close by. The Englischer Garten, one of the world's biggest city parks, starts just north of the old town. Creators suggest two to three days, plus a day trip if you can.

At Christmas the whole old town becomes one long market, from Karlsplatz to Marienplatz and the Rindermarkt, and there are a dozen more around it: a medieval market, a fairy-tale village in the Residenz courtyard for small children, a woodland market by the Chinese Tower, the pink LGBTQ+ market and the Tollwood winter festival on the Oktoberfest grounds. For families there are puppet shows, curling, an ice rink at Karlsplatz and the Deutsches Museum for wet days.

For solo travellers Munich is the easiest big city in Germany: police figures are the lowest of the big cities and creators felt safe everywhere, including alone at night. The things to plan for are crowds at Marienplatz (worst on Saturdays and at the noon Glockenspiel), real cold, early darkness, and the scruffier streets around the Hauptbahnhof, where many of the cheaper hotels are.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in Munich

Munich's Christmas market season is one of Germany's biggest: the old Christkindlmarkt on Marienplatz, traced back to 1642, spreads down the pedestrian zone, and a dozen themed markets surround it - medieval, fairy-tale, woodland, pink and the Tollwood winter festival. In 2026 Marienplatz runs from 20 November to 24 December. It is very crowded at weekends, but Munich has the lowest recorded crime of Germany's big cities, and police run uniformed and plain-clothes patrols and cameras across the markets.

Münchner Christkindlmarkt

Marienplatz and the pedestrian zone · 20 Nov - 24 Dec 2026 · Mon-Sat 10:00-21:00, Sun 10:00-20:00, 24 Dec 10:00-14:00 · closed 22 Nov · Great for kids

The original: over 130 stalls in front of the Neues Rathaus under a 25-30 m tree, running on through the pedestrian streets. Stalls sell ornaments, Lebkuchen and stollen; creators liked the bratwurst, Flammlachs (flame-grilled salmon in a roll, €9 in 2024 - long queue), apple fritters and Quarkbällchen. Crowds: The busiest market in the city - 'made Oktoberfest look like a walk in the park' on a Saturday. Fairly packed an hour after opening even on a Tuesday. Go early afternoon on a weekday; Rindermarkt and the Viktualienmarkt give breathing room.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds and timing
Marienplatz is shoulder to shoulder on Saturdays and in the evenings, and filled with phones at the 11:00 and 12:00 Glockenspiel. Early afternoon on a weekday is calmest; the Residenz is calm by day but packed at dinner time.
Via Chris and Sara, WalkingAround Caro, Life With Rachel and Christian, Mathers On The Map
Pickpockets: fewer, but police still warn
Pickpocketing fell 11% in 2025 to less than half its 2016 level, and police run uniformed and plain-clothes patrols, pickpocket squads and cameras at Marienplatz and the pedestrian zone. They still say thefts happen 'again and again' in market crowds: inner pockets, bags closed and in front, extra care in queues.
Via Deana and Phil
No knives on the markets
Munich police say carrying weapons and knives is banned on Christmas markets under weapons law. A permanent knife ban also covers the Alter Botanischer Garten by the station.
Source: Polizeipräsidium München / Stadt München
Cash, cards and deposits
The Marienplatz organiser says 80% of stalls take cards, some above a €1-10 minimum, but creators found many stalls cash only, and the medieval market was cash only in 2025. Mug deposits were €5 in 2025, and €10-15 at the medieval market. Toilets are at the Rathaus Prunkhof and the Marienplatz station.
Via Boozing Abroad | Sam and Chris, East-West Wander, Wolters World, valeska
Cold and dark
It's dark by about 4:20pm and creators saw freezing rain and heavy snow. Stalls have no heaters and few napkins - eating while you queue keeps you warm. The Residenz village and Tollwood have seating under cover.
Via Adventures of Matt and Nat, Wolters World, Ciara Gan, Jm Banquicio
Have a lost-child plan
Before you go in, agree a meeting point everyone can find - the Fish Fountain (Fischbrunnen) on Marienplatz is the classic one. A Wiley Fox QR wristband on each child means anyone who finds them can reach you straight away.
Source: Polizeipräsidium München / Stadt München
New Year's Eve
In 2025/26 fireworks were banned from 21:00 to 02:00 in the pedestrian zone from Marienplatz to Karlsplatz and at the Viktualienmarkt, and bangers were banned all day inside the Mittlerer Ring. About 6,000 people were at Marienplatz at midnight and press reports of the police balance called the night largely peaceful. 2026/27 rules are not out yet.
Source: Polizeipräsidium München / Stadt München

In 2026 the Marienplatz market runs 20 November to 24 December (closed Sunday 22 November; 24 December until 14:00); the Residenz village runs from 16 November to 22 December, Pink Christmas to 20 December, and most others to 23 December. Only Marienplatz and Schwabing open on Christmas Eve, and Tollwood is closed 24-30 December. Book hotels and restaurants early. Zone M day tickets rise from €10.10 to €10.80 on 1 January 2027.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

Munich has the lowest recorded crime of Germany's big cities. Police recorded 5,798 offences per 100,000 residents in the city in 2025, down 2.7%, against 13,233 in Berlin and 10,567 in Hamburg on the police's own comparison, and the Eurostat table below puts it lowest of eight big German cities for theft and robbery. Pickpocketing fell 11% in 2025 and street robbery by more than half. Two things went the other way: violent crime reached a ten-year high (up 2%), and recorded rapes rose 23%, though police say three in four involved people who knew each other. The areas police focus on are the Hauptbahnhof and the streets south of it, the Alter Botanischer Garten and Stachus. This rating is for the city as a whole, not street by street: German police do not publish street-level crime data.

The creators

Creators consistently call Munich one of the safest big cities they have visited. Rok Goes Around says pickpocketing is much rarer than in other large European cities, Happy to Wander says you won't be in danger even near the station, and Oriana Findlay, visiting alone, found even the Hofbräuhaus female friendly. The U-Bahn and S-Bahn were described as clean and safe. The complaints are crowds - Chris and Sara said a Saturday at the Marienplatz market made Oktoberfest look like a walk in the park - fast cyclists, cold weather, and prices. A local (German Insider) and Stefano Ferro both describe the Hauptbahnhof area as the scruffiest and noisiest part of the city.

What the official figures say

Figures are from the Munich police's Sicherheitsreport 2025 (published March 2026). The headline rate is for the City of Munich; all other counts cover the whole police area (city plus Landkreis München, about 1.86 million people). No street-level data is published. From 2025 the report uses Census 2022 population figures, so older rates are not directly comparable. Pickpocketing: 1,489 cases (down 10.6%, and less than half the 2016 level), with 340 on public transport (slightly up). Street robbery: 143 (down 59%). Violent crime: 5,133 (up 2%). Recorded offences in the Alter Botanischer Garten by the station fell 66% to 436 after alcohol, cannabis and knife bans from January 2025. Police say the Hauptbahnhof, a building site until well into the 2030s with about 450,000 people a day, still sees begging, street drinking and litter, but much less than a year earlier. Rates are per resident, so visitors and commuters push them up.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Hauptbahnhof & the southern Bahnhofsviertel
Take most care: Evenings and night

Munich's main station is a building site until well into the 2030s, with about 450,000 people passing through a day. Police say begging, street drinking, rough sleeping and litter are much less common than a year earlier, and their focus has moved to the streets south of the station (between Bayerstraße, Landwehrstraße and Sonnenstraße) and Nußbaumpark, where they now have camera towers. Drinking alcohol on the street around the station is banned. Creators found it scruffy rather than dangerous. Keep an eye on bags in the concourse, and use the main roads at night.

Via Stefano Ferro, German Insider, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo)
Alter Botanischer Garten
Take most care: After dark

The small park between the station and Stachus was Munich's biggest trouble spot: 1,284 offences in 2024. After alcohol, cannabis, weapon and knife bans, better lighting and cameras from January 2025, offences fell 66% to 436. It's still not a shortcut to take late at night - walk round via Karlsplatz. Kai Travel found a lot of litter around the fountain.

Via Kai Travel
Karlsplatz (Stachus)
Take most care: Late evening

The big square at the start of the pedestrian zone, home to the Eiszauber ice rink at Christmas. Police had complaints about loud groups of young people in 2024 and 2025 and now have a mobile camera tower as well as the fixed camera. Busy and well lit - just keep valuables close.

Via Greece and Beyond, Near From Home | Slow Travel Germany
Marienplatz at the Glockenspiel and on Saturdays
Take most care: 11:00 and 12:00 daily, and Saturday afternoons and evenings

The square fills with a wall of phones for the Glockenspiel, and on December Saturdays the market is shoulder to shoulder. Police warn of pickpockets in crowds and queues, and have uniformed and plain-clothes patrols and cameras at Marienplatz and the pedestrian zone. Go early on a weekday, and step out to the Rindermarkt or Viktualienmarkt for space.

Via Wolters World, Chris and Sara, Life With Rachel and Christian, Our Travel Place, Near From Home | Slow Travel Germany
Bike lanes everywhere
Take most care: Always

The most common warning from creators. Munich's cyclists are fast and bike lanes blend into the pavement, often in a slightly different colour. Look both ways before stepping off the kerb and keep children out of the red lanes.

Via Wolters World, Amy Zhong
The Eisbach in the Englischer Garten
Take most care: Any time

Watch the river surfers from the bridge, but the water is fast and cold. Eva Meloche's group struggled to get out after swimming; keep children well back from the edge.

Via Eva Meloche, MY Travel BF

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Plain-clothes ticket inspectors
Not a scam: MVV inspectors ride in normal clothes and show ID on board. Tickets must be validated (stamped) as well as bought, or you pay €60. Apps validate automatically.
Via Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), Rok Goes Around, Wolters World
Pickpockets in the crowd
Munich police warn that thefts happen 'again and again' in Christmas-market crowds. Keep valuables in inner pockets, bags closed and in front, and be careful in queues; ring 110 if you see something.
Via Deana and Phil
Deposits on everything
Mugs carry a Pfand, usually €5 in 2025, and plates €1. The medieval market charges €10-15 for its clay goblets, so a €8 drink costs €23 up front - you get it back when you return it.
Via Chris and Sara, East-West Wander, Wolters World
Taxis from the airport
About Munich says taxi scams are almost non-existent, but €100 or more from the airport is normal. The S-Bahn (S1/S8) with an Airport-City-Day-Ticket is far cheaper.
Via About Munich
Chapter Four · Where to base yourself
Neighbourhoods

Where to stay

Pick an area first, then a hotel. The old town is compact, so most bases are within a short walk of the markets.

Altstadt (around Marienplatz)
First-timers · Christmas markets

The best base for a short trip: everything in the old town is 10-15 minutes' walk, and the Christmas market is on your doorstep. It's also the most expensive area - Travel Tips Group quotes about €150-200 a night for three stars and €250-300 for four (2025) - and it is packed around Marienplatz on December weekends.

Via Travel Tips Group, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), MissTourist, Stefano Ferro
Near the Hauptbahnhof (Ludwigsvorstadt)
Budget · Arriving by train · Day trips

The cheapest hotels in the centre, 10 minutes' walk from the old town, with the airport S-Bahn and day-trip trains on the doorstep. Creators are split: Happy to Wander says you won't be in danger, while Stefano Ferro calls it the dodgiest, noisiest part of town and a local wouldn't live there with children. Police focus on the southern Bahnhofsviertel and the Alter Botanischer Garten, so choose a hotel on the north side or on the main road.

Via Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), Stefano Ferro, German Insider, Travel Tips Group, Our Travel Place
Lehel
Families · Quiet nights

Quiet, pretty and residential, between the old town and the Englischer Garten, and walkable to the Residenz and Marienplatz. Stefano Ferro recommends it for families; the catch is price and not much nightlife.

Via Stefano Ferro, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo)
Haidhausen
Families · Local feel

Across the Isar, quieter with a villagey feel, cafés and its own local Christmas market. A few minutes by S-Bahn from Marienplatz. Happy to Wander calls it a favourite and MissTourist says there's plenty for families.

Via Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), MissTourist, Senior Travel World, Near From Home | Slow Travel Germany
Schwabing & Maxvorstadt
Solo travellers · Museums · Couples

The university and museum quarter north of the centre, running into leafy Schwabing by the Englischer Garten. Slightly cheaper, lively with cafés and student bars, and on the U3/U6 for the Schwabing and Chinese Tower markets.

Via Travel Tips Group, Rok Goes Around, Senior Travel World, MissTourist
Glockenbachviertel & Gärtnerplatz
Solo travellers · Nightlife

Trendy, LGBTQ+ friendly and lively at night, close to Pink Christmas and the Viktualienmarkt. A little cheaper than the Altstadt, but further from the S-Bahn and noisy at weekends.

Via Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), City Zen, Stefano Ferro

What creators rate

Ten things worth your time

Ranked by how often creators recommend them. Most are within a 15-minute walk of Marienplatz; in December, see the sights by daylight and save the markets for the evening.

01

Marienplatz & the Glockenspiel

Altstadt · Great for kids

The main square under the neo-Gothic Neues Rathaus. Its Glockenspiel plays at 11:00 and 12:00 in winter - some creators call it underwhelming, but children love it. The Rathaus tower has a lift to a viewing platform, and the courtyard is free.

Viktualienmarkt

Chapter Seven · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around Munich

From the airport
The S1 and S8 run alternately, about every 10 minutes between them, taking around 40 minutes to the Hauptbahnhof (a bit less to Marienplatz on the S8). The Airport-City-Day-Ticket is €17.50 for one or €32.60 for a group of up to five (2026) and covers the rest of the day in the city. Don't board the occasional regional train to Regensburg. The Lufthansa Express bus is €13.
Via About Munich, Rob on Holiday
Tickets and fines
A Zone M single is €4.20, a day ticket €10.10 and a group day ticket for up to five €19.70 (2026); day tickets run to 6am the next morning. Stamp paper tickets before you travel - there are no barriers, inspectors wear normal clothes, and the fine is €60. MVV Swipe in the MVGO app works out the cheapest fare for you. Zone M day tickets rise to €10.80 on 1 January 2027.
Via Rob on Holiday, Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), About Munich, Anisha K G
Walking the markets
The main market runs through the pedestrian zone from Karlsplatz to Marienplatz and the Rindermarkt; the Residenz and medieval markets are 5-10 minutes north. Take the U3/U6 to Universität for the Chinese Tower or Münchner Freiheit for Schwabing, and U4/U5 to Theresienwiese for Tollwood.
Via Happy to Wander (Travel Tips & Inspo), Near From Home | Slow Travel Germany, Life With Rachel and Christian
Night travel
Public transport runs roughly 4am to 1am, with U-Bahn trains all night on Fridays, Saturdays and before holidays and night trams from about 1:30am (Rok Goes Around, 2023).
Via Rok Goes Around
Day trips by train
The Bayern-Ticket (€34 for one, €44 for two, up to five people plus children) covers regional trains across Bavaria for a day. Nuremberg's market is about an hour by ICE, Salzburg about 1h30-1h40. For Neuschwanstein, book the timed castle tour online (€21 + €2.50 fee): in winter the Marienbrücke viewpoint is often closed, paths are icy and it's dark by about 4:20pm.
Via Rob on Holiday, Wolters World, Travels of Sarah Fay, Meghan Elise

Quick tips

Sights by day, markets by night
In December it's dark by about 4:20pm. Do the towers, museums and park first and save the markets for when the lights come on.
Pack for real cold
Creators saw freezing rain, 44 cm of snow and feels-like temperatures of -16°C in early December. Market stalls have no heaters - layers, gloves and grippy shoes.
Book dinner
Restaurants were booked out even on a December Wednesday. Reserve for evenings, or make lunch your main meal - many places have cheaper lunch menus.
Sundays and holidays
Shops close on Sundays and on 25, 26 December and 1 January; the Viktualienmarkt is closed Sundays and holidays. The markets and museums stay open on Sundays (state museums are €1 on Sundays, most closed on Mondays).
Plan for Christmas itself
Most markets end on 20-23 December. Only Marienplatz and Schwabing open on 24 December, until 14:00. The Residenz, Deutsches Museum and Neuschwanstein are closed on 24-25 December.
Keep out of the bike lanes
Munich's cyclists are fast and the lanes blend into the pavement. Look both ways before stepping off the kerb, especially with children.

Chapter Eight · Local tips
The small things visitors worry about

Local tips for Munich

Every tip here was checked against an official or trusted source (MVV, Munich police, muenchen.de, Deutsche Bahn, gov.uk and others) on the date shown. Rules and prices change, so tap the link to double-check before you travel.

Paying for transport

Which ticket for a day of sightseeing?
A Zone M day ticket is €10.10 (2026) and runs until 6am the next morning; a Zone M single is €4.20. Buy at station machines (cards accepted) or in the MVV/MVGO app. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is there a family ticket?
The group day ticket covers up to five people for €19.70 in Zone M (2026) - cheaper than two adult day tickets plus children. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I avoid a €60 fine?
There are no ticket barriers: stamp paper tickets in the blue machine before you board. App tickets are validated automatically; inspectors wear normal clothes and show ID on board. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can the app pick the fare for me?
MVV Swipe in the MVGO app: swipe at the start and end of each trip and the fare is capped at the day-ticket price.
Which train from the airport?
Take the S1 or S8 (green S sign) - between them about every 10 minutes, around 40 minutes to the centre. Don't board the regional train to Regensburg.

Water & snacks

Where can we refill water bottles?
Munich tap water is safe to drink, and the Viktualienmarkt has free drinking fountains.
Christmas What can children drink at the markets?
Ask for Kinderpunsch - hot, non-alcoholic fruit punch - or hot chocolate. It comes in the same deposit mug as Glühwein.
Christmas Why did my drink cost €5 more?
Market mugs carry a deposit (Pfand), usually about €5 in 2025, plates about €1 and medieval-market goblets up to €15. Hand it back to any stall of the same market, or keep it as a souvenir.
Can we bring a picnic?
In traditional beer gardens you can bring your own food as long as you buy your drinks there - handy with children (most are closed in winter).

Toilets

Are public toilets free?
Usually not: expect 50 cents to €1 at stations and public WCs, and attendants in beer halls expect a small tip. Keep coins handy.
Where is a toilet near Marienplatz?
There is a paid WC in the Marienplatz station below the square; searching "WC" in Google Maps shows public toilets nearby.

Parks & play

Where can the children run around?
The Englischer Garten, the free Nymphenburg palace park and the Olympiapark all have space to run; Hirschgarten has deer to watch.
Christmas Where is the Christmas market for small children?
The Christmas village in the Residenz courtyard has a fairy-tale forest and puppet shows aimed at young children.

Money & tipping

Christmas Do I need cash?
Yes. The Marienplatz organiser says about 80% of its stalls take cards, but many smaller stalls, bakeries such as Café Frischhut and toilets are cash only. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I tip?
Card machines rarely have a tip button. Tell the server the total you want to pay - about 5-10% or rounded up - for example "18" on a €16.50 bill.

Phones & data

Which apps should I download?
The MVV or MVGO app for routes and tickets; check the end-of-line station on the platform sign to be sure of your direction.
Do I need a local SIM?
A Europe-wide eSIM bought before travel works fine; UK plans may charge roaming, so check before you go.

Safety habits

Anything to watch when walking?
Bike lanes blend into the pavement and cyclists ride fast, even in snow. Look both ways before crossing them and keep children out of the red lanes.
Christmas How do I dodge the worst crowds?
Visit markets on a weekday daytime or early afternoon; Saturdays and the 11:00 and 12:00 Glockenspiel are the busiest times at Marienplatz.
Christmas Pickpockets at the markets?
Keep valuables in an inner pocket and bags zipped and in front in crowds and queues. Call 110 for police, 112 for fire and ambulance.
Christmas Is it slippery?
Icy paving and rain made many people slip at Marienplatz - wear grippy, waterproof shoes.

Local ways

Will shops be open on Sunday?
Shops and supermarkets close on Sundays and on 25-26 December and 1 January, except in stations and the airport. Christmas markets stay open.
How do I eat Weisswurst?
Order it before noon with sweet mustard and a pretzel, and peel the skin rather than eating it.
Christmas Do we need to book dinner?
Yes in December - restaurants and beer halls fill up, even midweek. Expect shared tables and order everything at once.
Christmas When is the Marienplatz market open?
20 November - 24 December 2026: Mon-Sat 10:00-21:00, Sun 10:00-20:00, and 24 December until 14:00. Checked 2026-09 ↗
