Chapter One · The City

New York is easier to plan than it looks: Manhattan is a grid, the subway runs 24 hours, and you tap your bank card to ride. The big decision is where to sleep. Creators agree Midtown is the simplest first-time base but also the most expensive, and several now point to Long Island City, the Financial District or Brooklyn for better value a short ride away. Short-term rentals have been effectively banned since a 2023 law, so plan on a hotel.

For families, many of the best things are free - Central Park, the Staten Island Ferry past the Statue of Liberty, the High Line, the Brooklyn Bridge and the Public Library - and the Natural History Museum is a favourite with every creator who took children. Up to three children under 44 inches (about 112 cm) ride the subway free with a paying adult.

For solo travellers it is a city that never really stops. The risks are ordinary big-city ones - theft, scams and the odd unsettling moment on a late train - and they cluster in predictable places. This guide shows you where, using the same NYPD data that powers the Wiley Fox map.

Chapter Two · Christmas
The festive season

Christmas in New York

From Thanksgiving to New Year, New York is at its most magical - and its busiest and most expensive. More than seven million visitors arrive between Thanksgiving and Christmas, and hotel prices can be three to five times higher than the rest of the year. The trick every creator repeats: go early in the morning, on weekdays, and book ahead.

Staying safe in the Christmas crowds

Crowds at the tree and on Fifth Avenue
In December, pavements around Rockefeller Center are body-to-body and police close roads to manage the crowds. That area already records about 2,200 crimes a month, almost half theft. Go at dawn or on a weekday, carry little, and keep bags zipped and in front.
Via Katelyn DeWitt, Manps On The Map, Danny Rayes, Sarah Funk
Pickpockets at the markets
Busy stalls are ideal for pickpockets - one creator saw a wallet taken from a shopper at Union Square as they paid. The circle around Bryant Park records about 2,400 crimes a month and the most pickpocket and theft-from-the-person complaints in our sample (about 64 a month). Pay with your phone or card, and don't hang anything off a backpack.
Via Mohuya Khan
Have a lost-child plan
Before you enter a market, rink or parade crowd, agree a meeting point you can all see - a named stall, the rink entrance or a shop front. Creators describe big holiday crowds with no toilets and people camping for a view. A Wiley Fox QR wristband on each child means anyone who finds them can reach you straight away.
Via Sarah Funk
Dress for the cold
Creators quote December highs from about 42F to 58F (6-14C) and lows around freezing, with wind that bites. Wear layers, a windproof coat, hat and gloves. They disagree on boots: one says trainers are fine, another packs waterproof boots. Sunset is around 4pm, most park toilets close around then, and subway steps get slippery in wet weather.
Via The Quick Advisors, Jon Barr Shorts, Dicas Nova York
New Year's Eve in Times Square
It's free, but viewing pens opened at 3pm in 2025, with no toilets, no re-entry and all bags searched - backpacks, umbrellas and alcohol aren't allowed. Expect hundreds of thousands of people and a long, freezing wait. Not one for young children; Sarah Funk says skip it. Hotel viewing packages ran $500 to over $1,000.
Via Sarah Funk, Jon Barr Shorts
Pedicabs and Santa photos
Decorated pedicabs around Rockefeller Center charged $7-10 a minute in 2025, and costumed Santas expect payment for photos. Agree a price first, or just say no.
Via Jon Barr Shorts

Book hotels as early as you can - one creator suggests up to a year ahead - and Radio City, Santaland, rinks and restaurants weeks in advance. Most 2026 dates, including the tree lighting, aren't published yet: markets usually open from late November, so check official sites; Bryant Park runs 28 Oct 2026 - 28 Feb 2027. Many Chinatown restaurants stay open on Christmas Day.

Chapter Three · Safety
What the data and the creators say

How it feels, and what the numbers show

The data

New York is far safer than its film reputation and most visits pass without trouble. The main risk for visitors is theft - pickpocketing, phone theft and street scams - and it clusters in the busiest parts of Midtown, especially around Times Square. Serious violence against tourists is rare; take normal big-city care late at night, on quiet streets and on near-empty trains.

The creators

Most creators we reviewed say New York feels safer than people expect, and one who rode the subway more than 50 times in four weeks had no problems. The repeated warnings are practical: keep phones and wallets in front, ignore anyone offering something 'free' in Times Square, and avoid empty subway cars. Creators split on late nights: some ride the subway at any hour, while two women creators take a car or bus after about 11pm to midnight.

Areas to take extra care

None of these are no-go areas. Know the pattern, and plan your timing and your route home.

Times Square
Take most care: All day, busiest late afternoon and evening

About 2,300 recorded crimes a month within a mile, almost half of them theft, with around 60 pickpocket and theft-from-the-person complaints a month. Creators say almost every tourist scam happens here: costumed characters charging $20 or more for photos, 'free' CDs and bracelets that end in a demand for money. It is not unsafe - it is busy. Go for a show or a photo, keep your phone in front, and keep walking.

2,291 crimes a month nearby · top: theft, violence, harassment
Via Sarah Funk, Wolters World, Jon Barr, Exotic Vacation, Jon Barr Shorts, Manps On The Map
Rockefeller Center & Fifth Avenue
Take most care: Late November to early January, evenings and weekends

About 2,200 recorded crimes a month, almost half theft. At Christmas the pavements are body-to-body, roads are closed for crowds and photo queues by the tree can take hours. Decorated pedicabs here charged $7 to $10 a minute in 2025, and people in Santa costumes expect payment for photos.

2,170 crimes a month nearby · top: theft, violence, harassment
Via Danny Rayes, Manps On The Map, Katelyn DeWitt, Jon Barr Shorts
Herald Square / Penn Station
Take most care: All day, especially around the stations

One of the highest counts in our sample - about 2,400 a month, nearly half theft. Outside Penn Station, Moynihan Train Hall and Port Authority, strangers pick up bags uninvited and then demand a tip. Hold on to your luggage and say no firmly.

2,375 crimes a month nearby · top: theft, violence, harassment
Via Jon Barr
Union Square
Take most care: Holiday market season and weekends

About 1,900 recorded crimes a month and more than half are theft, one of the highest theft shares we found. One creator saw a wallet stolen from a shopper paying at a busy market stall. Keep wallets in front pockets and nothing hanging off the outside of a backpack.

1,933 crimes a month nearby · top: theft, violence, harassment
Via Mohuya Khan
Battery Park & the ferry terminals
Take most care: Daytime, when the Statue of Liberty boats run

Recorded crime is low - about 700 a month - but this is where fake ticket sellers work. The Staten Island Ferry is free, so never buy a ticket for it, and only Statue City Cruises sells genuine Liberty and Ellis Island tickets.

688 crimes a month nearby · top: theft, other, violence
Via Jon Barr, Exotic Vacation, Sarah Funk, Jon Barr Shorts
Central Park after dark
Take most care: Evenings, and all day on the southern edge

Central Park is a highlight by day. One creator says locals advise not going in after dark. Along the southern edge, pedicabs charge by the minute - one creator reports about $150 for 10 minutes - so check the posted price first. Another creator advises against the horse-drawn carriages on welfare grounds.

1,638 crimes a month nearby · top: theft, violence, harassment

Scams and tricks creators keep seeing

Costumed characters & 'free' gifts
In Times Square, nothing is free: CDs, bracelets, 'monks' with donation books and photos with costumed characters all end in a demand for money - $20 or more in one creator's experience. Don't make eye contact; keep walking.
Via Wolters World, Jon Barr, Exotic Vacation, Jon Barr Shorts, Manps On The Map
Pedicab pricing
Pedicabs in Midtown and around Central Park charge by the minute - $7 to $10 a minute near Rockefeller Center in 2025. Agree the total price before you sit down, or walk.
Via Wolters World, Jon Barr Shorts
Fake ferry & Statue tickets
Sellers around Battery Park offer tickets for the Staten Island Ferry, which is free, and 'Statue tours' that don't land on Liberty Island. Buy only from Statue City Cruises.
Via Jon Barr, Exotic Vacation, Sarah Funk, Jon Barr Shorts
Airport ride touts
Anyone approaching you in arrivals, or holding an 'Uber' sign, is not a licensed driver. Use the official taxi line - a real yellow cab shows ID and 'TLC' on the plate - or book in the app.
Via Jon Barr, Sarah Funk, Jon Barr Shorts, Mohuya Khan
Marked-up show tickets
Street sellers and look-alike reseller sites charge far more for Broadway and observation-deck tickets. Buy from the show's own site, the TKTS booth, or official lotteries.
Via Jon Barr, Mohuya Khan
Phone and card distraction
Two tricks one creator describes: someone selling sweets asks to borrow your phone for a payment app, then drains your accounts; and at ATMs, cash is dropped behind you to distract you while your card is swapped. Never hand over your phone; cover the keypad.
Via Jon Barr
Exchange booths and shop ATMs
Street currency exchanges give poor rates with hidden fees, and ATMs inside cash-only shops add hefty charges. Bring a little cash and use a bank ATM.
Via Sarah Funk, Wolters World

Chapter Seven · Getting around & costs
Before you go

Getting around New York

From the airports
From JFK, the AirTrain plus the subway gets you to Manhattan for under $15 in about an hour; the AirTrain plus the Long Island Rail Road from Jamaica is quicker (as little as 20 minutes) and has luggage racks. From LaGuardia a free shuttle bus runs to the subway. From Newark take NJ Transit to the last stop, New York Penn Station - not Newark Penn. A taxi or Uber costs around $100, and surge pricing can push Uber higher; use the official taxi line.
Via Sarah Funk, Jon Barr Shorts, Nomac Guides, Exotic Vacation, Traveling with Tim
Tap to pay
No MetroCard needed: tap a contactless card or phone at the subway gate or on a local bus. A ride is $3, and after 12 paid rides in seven days the rest are free, so a week tops out at $35 (MTA, Sept 2026). Each person needs their own card or device for the cap. There's no need to tap out.
Via Jon Barr, Nomac Guides, Sarah Funk, Jon Barr Shorts, Pictures of New York
Subway know-how
Stand well back from the platform edge. If you want crew nearby, wait by the black-and-white striped board mid-platform - it lines up with the conductor's car. An empty car is empty for a reason (often no heating or a bad smell). Platforms and trains have help points, and busy stations usually have police. Wet or snowy steps get slippery.
Via Mohuya Khan, thefunsizedfoodie, Sarah Funk, The Megan Daily, Jon Barr Shorts
Late at night
Creators disagree. Several ride at any hour and say normal awareness is enough; two women creators avoid the subway after about 11pm to midnight, when trains are fewer and platforms emptier, and take a car or a bus with the driver present instead. If you're alone, plan the last leg home before you go out.
Via Wolters World, Nomac Guides, Sarah Funk
Ferries
The Staten Island Ferry is free, runs 24 hours about every 30 minutes and passes the Statue of Liberty - sit on the right on the way out. The NYC Ferry from Pier 11 (Wall Street) to DUMBO takes under five minutes; creators quoted $4 in 2025 and $4.50 in 2026. The Governors Island ferry was $5 return in 2026.
Via Traveling with Tim, Nomac Guides, Jon Barr Shorts, Jon Barr
Cheap scenic rides
The Roosevelt Island Tramway is a three-minute cable car over the East River for the price of a subway ride. Downtown, the free Downtown Connection bus loops past Battery Park, the Seaport and the 9/11 Memorial (10am to 7.30pm in 2026). Hop-on buses crawl at rush hour - ride 9-11am or 2-3pm.
Via Island Hopper TV, Nomac Guides, Leyla Tavas
Taxis and ride-hailing
A yellow taxi is free only when its roof light is lit. Avoid taxis and ride-hailing at rush hour - traffic is gridlocked and surge pricing can make an Uber twice the price of a cab.
Via Jon Barr Shorts, Wolters World
Don't drive
Every creator who mentions it says no: tolls on most bridges and tunnels, a 25 mph limit, no right turn on red, and hotel parking charged daily. Walk, and wear broken-in shoes - you'll do a lot of it.
Via Mileage Mike, Sarah Funk, Travel Tips Group, Jon Barr Shorts

Quick tips

Book the first slot
Observation decks, the Statue of Liberty ferry and even Times Square are calm first thing. Creators who booked the earliest slot had rooms almost to themselves.
Step aside to stop
New Yorkers walk fast. Pull to the side of the pavement for photos or maps, and know your order before you reach the counter.
Carry a little cash
Some carts and bodegas take cards only above a minimum, many Chinatown restaurants are cash only, and shop ATMs can turn a $3 snack into $8.
Don't eat in Times Square
Prices are higher and it's mostly chains. Walk a few blocks to Ninth Avenue in Hell's Kitchen for local places.
Check free museum times
Several museums have free or pay-what-you-wish slots, such as MoMA on Friday evenings (reserve ahead). Check each museum's site for current times.
Book a hotel, not a flat
Short-term rentals have been effectively banned since a 2023 law. Stick to hotels, and book early for December.

Chapter Eight · Local tips
The small things visitors worry about

Local tips for New York

Every tip here was checked against an official or trusted source on the date shown. Rules and prices change, so tap the link to double-check before you travel.

Paying for transport

Do I need to buy a MetroCard?
No need to queue for a ticket: tap your contactless bank card, phone or watch at the OMNY reader on the subway or local bus. The fare is $3 a ride (checked Sept 2026). Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is there a weekly fare cap?
With OMNY, once you've paid for 12 rides in seven days the rest are free, so weekly spend on subways and local buses tops out at $35 (checked Sept 2026). Use the same card or phone every time. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Do my kids pay on the subway?
Up to three children under 44 inches (about 112 cm) tall ride subways and local buses free with a paying adult, so don't tap for very small ones. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Can we all use one card?
Use the same card or phone for both legs of a journey to get your free transfer, and note that fare-capping is tracked per card, so don't swap between cards or devices. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What about the commuter trains like LIRR and Metro-North?
For the LIRR or Metro-North, buy your ticket in the TrainTime app or at a machine beforehand: buying from the conductor on board adds a surcharge. Children under 5 travel free. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Water & snacks

Do I need to buy bottled water?
Skip the bottled water: New York's tap water is safe to drink and comes from upstate reservoirs, so just refill a reusable bottle at your hotel or a park fountain. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Will grocery snacks have tax added?
Picking up snacks and drinks from a grocery store or deli? Most unprepared food isn't charged sales tax, so the shelf price is usually what you pay, unlike restaurant meals. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Toilets

When are park loos open?
Most NYC Parks public toilets are open roughly 8am to 4pm daily, and later in some seasons, so don't leave it until dusk. Look for a café or hotel lobby after that. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Where can I find a decent loo in Midtown?
Bryant Park's toilets are a Midtown favourite, kept smart with attendants, and Grand Central Terminal, libraries and big department stores are handy backups when you need one. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Are there toilets at the Bryant Park Winter Village?
Yes, public toilets are available throughout Bryant Park during Winter Village hours, so you can plan a skate or shopping stop without hunting for one. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Are there toilets at the Times Square New Year's Eve crowd?
There are no portable toilets in the Times Square New Year's Eve viewing area, and if you leave you can't get back to your spot, so go before you enter the pens and limit what you drink. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Parks & play

Can we use any NYC playground?
Playgrounds are free, but check the rules sign at the entrance: bikes, scooters, skateboards and pets are not allowed, and at many playgrounds adults can only go in with a child. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Is the Bryant Park skating rink free?
The Rink at Bryant Park's Winter Village has free admission if you bring your own skates, but you still need to reserve a free ticket in advance. Hiring skates costs extra and prices are on the official site. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas Can we browse Winter Village without paying?
Entry to Bryant Park's Winter Village holiday shops is free. The 2026 to 27 season is listed as starting 28 October, so it's easy to fit in on the way to other Midtown sights. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Money & tipping

How much should I tip in restaurants?
Tipping is expected: around 20% is standard in sit-down restaurants, and under 18% usually signals you weren't happy. Tips are not included in menu prices. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Why is my bill higher than the menu prices?
Prices on tags and menus don't include sales tax, which is added at the till. New York City's combined rate is 8.875%. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is there tax on clothes?
Clothing and footwear costing under $110 per item aren't charged sales tax in NYC, so that pair of trainers might be cheaper than the maths suggests. Items at $110 or more pay the full rate. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Could a service charge already be on the bill?
Always check the bill: large groups sometimes have a gratuity or service charge added automatically, so you may not need to tip on top. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Phones & data

Will I get stung by roaming charges?
Check your UK provider's roaming charges for the USA before you fly, set a spend limit, and turn off data roaming if you don't have a bundle. Use Wi-Fi where you can. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Is there a cheaper way to get data?
A local SIM or an eSIM for your destination can be cheaper than roaming, and it's worth downloading maps and shows on Wi-Fi before you go. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Will my UK chargers work?
US sockets run at 120V and take Type A and B plugs, so pack a travel adaptor. Most phone and laptop chargers cope with 120V, but check the label on hairdryers and straighteners first. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Safety habits

Who do I call in an emergency?
For emergencies (police, fire, medical), dial 911. For non-emergency city help, call or text 311, which has interpreters in over 175 languages, 24/7. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I keep my things safe on the subway?
On the subway, keep bags in front of you and stay aware of your phone and belongings, especially on crowded trains and platforms. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Where should I stand on the platform?
Stand well back from the platform edge, behind the yellow line, and look for the black and white striped board overhead near the middle of the platform to board near the conductor. Checked 2026-09 ↗
What should I do when crossing the road?
Even with a walk signal, look around for turning vehicles, especially left-turners: NYC DOT says three in four pedestrian crashes happen at intersections. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas What should I bring to Times Square on New Year's Eve?
Leave backpacks, large bags, umbrellas and alcohol behind, as all bags are searched. Viewing areas open at 3pm (2025 times, so check again this year) and fill first come, first served. Checked 2026-09 ↗

Local ways

Do I need anything before flying from the UK?
British visitors need an approved ESTA before flying. Apply only on the official esta.cbp.dhs.gov site (the fee was $40 from 30 Sept 2025), and apply well ahead of your trip. Checked 2026-09 ↗
How do I stay safe around bikes and e-bikes?
Look both ways in bike lanes as well as on the road: cyclists follow the same traffic rules as drivers, e-bikes are capped at 15mph and should stay off pavements and yield to pedestrians. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas When is the Rockefeller tree lit this year?
The 2026 Rockefeller Center tree lighting date hasn't been published yet, so check rockefellercenter.com nearer the time and don't plan flights around a guess. Checked 2026-09 ↗
Christmas How do I see the Christmas windows without the crush?
The famous Fifth Avenue and Herald Square window displays are busiest earlier in the evening, so go once most tourists have gone to bed for a closer look. Check retailers' sites for opening dates. Checked 2026-09 ↗
